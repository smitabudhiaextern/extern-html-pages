The Five Parts of
Your Dashboard Page

A dashboard is built from five kinds of building block. Here is what each one looks like on the page.

The whole page

What each block on the page is called

Part 1 of 5

What a <div> does

A <div> is a box that holds things. The dashboard uses five of them, one for each of the five numbers across the top of the page.

The code

<div class="kpi-card">$61,085</div>

class="kpi-card" is the box's name. The styling at the top of the file uses that name to give the box its white background and rounded corners.

How it renders

Boxes sit inside boxes. In the template, the card is one div, and the number and the label are two smaller divs inside it.

Parts 2 and 3 of 5

What <h2> and <p> do

<h2> is a block title. It names the section under it and renders large and bold.

<p> is a paragraph. It holds one sentence of body text at normal reading size.

The code

<h2>Top 10 Products by Revenue</h2>
<p>Revenue is concentrated in three products.</p>

How it renders

The Key Findings block holds three separate <p> tags, so each of your three findings goes in its own paragraph.

Part 4 of 5

What a <table> does

The product leaderboard is a <table>. Inside it, <tr> is one row and <td> is one cell in that row. Five cells make up one product line.

The code, two rows of it

<tr><td>1</td><td>Dwellings of Eldervale 2nd Edition: Standard</td><td>$13,323.56</td><td>129</td><td>$103.28</td></tr>
<tr><td>2</td><td>Dwellings of Eldervale - LEGENDARY Upgrade Kit</td><td>$12,514.05</td><td>175</td><td>$71.51</td></tr>

Filling the leaderboard means swapping the cell contents for your own top 10 from dashboard_leaderboard. The row and cell tags stay exactly as they are.

Part 5 of 5

What a <canvas> does

A <canvas> is an empty space on the page. Nothing shows up inside it until a charting tool draws there.

The code

<canvas id="revenueDistChart"></canvas>

The id is the space's name. The chart code looks that name up to find out where to draw.

Before and after

The tool that draws into these spaces is Chart.js, and it is the next module. The template already holds two canvas spaces, one for the doughnut and one for the bar chart.

Your job

How to fill in the template

What ships in the file

$<!-- STUDENT: total_revenue -->61,085

After you edit it

$<!-- STUDENT: total_revenue -->your number

The comment stays put. It marks the spot for you and does not show on the page. You are filling in blanks in a page that is already built.
