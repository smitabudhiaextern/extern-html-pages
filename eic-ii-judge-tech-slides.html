Judge the technology

Does Phaidra beat the current way?

  • The old way: cooling controls a technician sets by hand. Saves about 2–5%.
  • Phaidra: AI software that adjusts the cooling automatically. Claims it saves 25–30% (the company's own claim).
  • If that holds up, it's a big enough gain that a data center would actually switch.

But it's a claim, not a fact yet

  • The 25–30% saving is Phaidra's own number, with no outside proof yet.
  • The Google story (that Google DeepMind's version cut Google's cooling energy 40%) is also the company's claim, with no outside proof.
  • Treat a company's own numbers as claims to check, not facts. That's what makes a careful analyst, not a cheerleader.

Name where it doesn't work

  • Every technology has limits, and a careful analyst names them.
  • Good fit: big AI data centers that already have the sensors the software needs.
  • Weak fit: small sites with no sensors to feed it.
  • Saying so isn't negative — it's what an investor expects, and it shows you understand the tech, not just the pitch.
