Optional challenges

For students who finish the core path.

Factory holidays

Plan around Diwali, Chinese New Year and Kurban Bayram, when factories slow down or close for weeks.

Tariffs

Compare the landed cost when duty rates differ by the country the rug is made in.

Rugs from India or Turkey

Most US rugs come from India and Turkey. From India, the choice is through Suez or around Africa. Turkey ships straight to the East Coast.

None of these are needed to score the core path.

How the team works

Tools do the math. Agents choose, trade off and explain.

The Planner reads one supplier’s week, asks the Demand Brief what is happening, hands the decision to the track’s agent, has the plan checked, and has the memo written. Showing: On the Water.

Planner AGENT
Reads one item-week and decides who to call. Checks the chosen plan with the plan timeline tool, which tests it under 27 swings in rates, fuel and demand.
Demand Brief TOOLS
One call that works out what is happening: sales against forecast, the weeks ahead, days of cover with a few days of buffer, and how much to order. A Demand Analyst agent steps in only when a spike is not a repeat.

Six weeks

Four projects. One agent team.

WEEKS 1–2
Meet n8n

Build a first agent, install n8n on your own computer, and import the data.

WEEKS 2–3
The Demand Desk

Sales Check, Stock Runway, Order Need and a Demand Analyst: what is happening to this item, and how much will it need?

WEEKS 3–4
Your track

The core agent for your problem and its tools. Starter kits cover the simple lookups. Extras are open to anyone who wants them.

WEEKS 4–6
The Planner and the readout

Wire everything under a Planner, add the Plan Checker and Memo Writer, fix the misses, and present a recommendation.

Nothing gets thrown away. The Planner calls the workflows built in the Demand Desk and in your track.

What students leave with

A recommendation a supply planner can read in five minutes.

A working team of agents
A Planner that calls your tools and agents, with a checker that never makes a plan riskier.
A one-page recommendation to a supplier
What to do, by when, what it costs, and what could go wrong.
A short readout
The strongest students present their recommendation and how they built it.
How it’s scored
One number per item

Total cost of the team’s plan (empty shelf, expedite premium, freight, trucking) minus the best plan’s total. Zero is perfect.

A fair comparison

The obvious rule: order when cover falls to the lead time, on the cheapest quoted route. Students see where their team does better and where it ties.

The house rule

When the data is missing, the team says “can’t tell yet”. An honest unknown is a correct answer. Scoring uses items students haven’t seen.

How we handle this responsibly

Every product, supplier and number is fictional or illustrative. The freight series is synthetic. We use public information only, and nothing here describes Wayfair’s own systems. The plan weighs the supplier’s costs against lost sales on the shelf.
