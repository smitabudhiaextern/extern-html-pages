Four Decisions Before You Build

The principle is settled: pre-calculate the answers into one table so the dashboard reads instead of computes. Four choices are still yours to make, and none of them has a single correct answer. You will record all four in the two Tasks that follow, along with your reason for each.

Decision 1 of 4

Wide or long?

Wide means one row with one column per KPI, so SELECT * FROM dashboard_metrics; returns everything at once. Adding a sixth KPI later means rewriting the CREATE statement.

Long means one row per KPI, with a metric_name column and a value column. Adding a KPI is just inserting a row, but the dashboard has to pick each value out by name.

Go wide. Your KPI list is short and stable, so the rewrite cost rarely comes due. Say why in your submission rather than just stating the choice.

Decision 2 of 4

What does "top" mean?

Two calls go into dashboard_leaderboard. How many products it holds, top 5 or top 10, and what it ranks them by, revenue or units sold. Those two settings produce different leaderboards from the same data, so pick one pair and justify it.

Ranking by units flips the top two. The LEGENDARY Upgrade Kit sold more copies, 175 against 129, but earned less revenue than the 2nd Ed Standard. Your choice changes what the page says.

Decision 3 of 4

How do channels roll up?

The raw traffic data holds one row per referrer: facebook, google, instagram, bing, boardgamegeek and more. Twenty slivers on a chart is unreadable, so dashboard_channels groups referrers into a handful of channels. Where you draw those group lines is your call.

Total pageviews come to 333,550. Whether boardgamegeek, at 1,510 views, earns its own Community channel or gets folded into Other is exactly the call you have to make and defend.

Decision 4 of 4

View or table?

You make this call separately for each of the three objects. A table stores the computed result, so reads are fast but the numbers are a snapshot from the last rebuild. A view re-runs its query on every read, so it is always current but does the work each time.

You already built one view in Project 4, clean_checkouts, so the mechanics are familiar.

A heavy aggregation you read constantly wants to be a table. A light slice that has to be current can be a view. Make the call for each of your three objects and be ready to say why.
