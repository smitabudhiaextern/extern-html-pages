From Assumptions to a Full Projection

Seven steps. Five percentages. One complete income statement.

Step 1 of 7

Step 1: Project Revenue

Revenue is the spine. Every other line flows from it, so you project it first.

Formula
Projected Revenue = Prior Year Revenue × (1 + Growth Rate)
FY26 Calculation
FY25 Revenue: $1,140,640K  ·  FY26 Growth Rate: 30%
$1,140,640 × 1.30 = $1,482,832K

For FY27–FY30, apply the same formula each year using that year's growth rate (15%, 10%, 7%, 5%) on the prior year's projected revenue.

Step 2 of 7

Step 2: Project Gross Profit

After projecting revenue, the next line item on the income statement is COGS. But we won't project that directly. Instead, we go straight to Gross Profit. Why? Because we already set a gross margin assumption. Gross margin tells you what % of revenue becomes Gross Profit. If it's 23%, every $100 of revenue produces $23 of Gross Profit. Multiply revenue by the margin to get Gross Profit. COGS is whatever's left over.

Formula
Projected Gross Profit = Projected Revenue × Gross Margin %
FY26 Calculation
$1,482,832K × 23.0% = $341,051K

COGS falls out as the difference: $1,482,832 − $341,051 = $1,141,781K

Step 3 of 7

Step 3: Project SG&A

After projecting Gross Profit, the next line item on the income statement is SG&A. This one we project directly, using our SG&A % assumption. How? We already set an SG&A % assumption (8.5% for FY26). SG&A grows with the business: bigger revenue means more salespeople, more software, more office space. Projecting it as a % of revenue captures that. If SG&A is 8.5% of revenue, every $100 of revenue produces $8.50 of SG&A. Multiply revenue by the SG&A % to get the dollar amount.

Formula
Projected SG&A = Projected Revenue × SG&A %
FY26 Calculation
$1,482,832K × 8.5% = $126,041K
Step 4 of 7

Step 4: Calculate Operating Income

The next line on the income statement is Operating Income. This one isn't an assumption we set. It's a calculation. Why? Think of the income statement as a waterfall. Revenue is what comes in. Subtract COGS to get Gross Profit (what's left after paying for cost of services). Subtract SG&A to get Operating Income (what's left after paying for overhead too). We already have Gross Profit ($341,051K) and SG&A ($126,041K) from the last two steps. Operating Income is just the subtraction.

Formula
Projected Operating Income = Projected Gross Profit − Projected SG&A
FY26 Calculation
$341,051K − $126,041K = $215,011K

In this model, D&A is embedded inside COGS and SG&A — so Operating Income already reflects D&A.

Step 5 of 7

Step 5: Project D&A

D&A is the last of our 5 assumptions. We project it the same way as SG&A: multiply revenue by the D&A %.

Formula
Projected D&A = Projected Revenue × D&A %
FY26 Calculation
$1,482,832K × 0.5% = $7,414K

D&A is shown supplementally so you can compute EBITDA in Step 6 — it is already embedded in COGS and SG&A above.

Step 6 of 7

Step 6: Calculate EBITDA

The next line is EBITDA. This is another calculation, not an assumption. What's EBITDA? It stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. In plain English: it's operating profit with D&A added back. Analysts use it as a clean way to compare profitability across companies. In this model, D&A is buried inside COGS and SG&A. So to get to EBITDA, we add D&A back on top of Operating Income.

Formula
EBITDA = Operating Income + D&A
FY26 Calculation
$215,011K + $7,414K = $222,425K

Because D&A is buried inside COGS and SG&A in this model, we add it back on top of Operating Income to arrive at EBITDA.

Step 7 of 7

Step 7: Project Capex

Capex is the last of our 5 assumptions. It doesn't sit on the income statement (it lives on the cash flow statement). We project it the same way as SG&A and D&A: multiply revenue by the Capex %.

Formula
Projected Capex = Projected Revenue × Capex %
FY26 Calculation
$1,482,832K × 2.0% = $29,657K

Capex feeds the cash flow statement, not the income statement — but it is driven by the same revenue base as all other assumptions.
