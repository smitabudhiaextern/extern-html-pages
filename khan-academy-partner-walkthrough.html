The shape of it

Four projects, seven weeks

A student joins as an analyst on your team and finishes by presenting two partnerships they would chase. Here is what each project asks of them.

Week 1 · Project 1

Reverse-Engineering Khan Academy's Partnerships

Work out how Khan Academy pays for itself. Take apart the College Board deal. Learn the four shapes a deal can take, and then learn what this team turns down.

They hand in: the rails in their own words, and one partner on one screen
Week 2 · Project 2

Cracking the Free-to-Paid Playbook

Study how Wikimedia, Coursera, Duolingo and Mozilla earn from free products, then shortlist the money models Khan Academy could borrow, each with a real precedent behind it.

They hand in: a shortlist of two or three models
Weeks 3 to 4 · Project 3

Finding Two Deals Worth Pitching

Learn what is off the table and why. Use AI research to find names nobody handed them, then cut the list to two partnerships and work out what each is worth.

They hand in: two partnerships, sized, with the arithmetic shown
Weeks 5 to 7 · Project 4

Making the Pitch

Build the case for both deals, argue the partner's side from their seat, name the risks, and turn all of it into a deck that says which one goes first.

They hand in: an eight-slide pitch deck

Just as important

What we chose not to publish

Your answers shaped the program as much by what they fenced off as by what they supplied. These are things you told us that appear nowhere a student can see.

"There is some work being done with with College Board to create a personalized plan based on SAT scores, so no reason to mention that."

Zero mentions, anywhere. It is on our never-publish list. We also rewrote the College Board module to describe the old score-import feature in the past tense only, and removed links to the two public College Board pages that still describe it in the present tense, so students are not sent looking for it.

"We currently don't have any revenue share partnerships, but we're open to these because have a small sales team."

Not used. Students learn "revenue share" as the name of a deal shape, and nothing anywhere says Khan Academy has none, is open to one, or has a small sales team.

"Microsoft gives us Azure credits and Google is giving us money to complete some Literacy work."

Neither used as you put it. The word "credits" is banned in our production notes in favour of Microsoft's own published wording. And Google is shown to students as an untyped deal with no money direction, even though you told us money moves, because no public source says so. The note on that page reads: "Do not close it without a source a student can open."

"some partners give us money (like College Board and AAMC) to maintain/update the test prep courses"

The direction is never stated. Students are told the College Board deal's money terms were never published and that working without them is part of the job. AAMC appears only with wording taken from AAMC's own page.
