Pick the Right Number — what's a KPI? (text version)

A KPI is the one number that tells you whether something is working. The skill is picking the right number for the question you are asking.

  1. Tony wants to know if the pizza shop is making money. Best single number? Answer: Total dollars earned (revenue). Followers and menu size don't tell you if you're profitable.
  2. Tony's Hawaiian pizza page gets tons of visitors but almost nobody orders. Which number flags the problem? Answer: Conversion rate — of the people who looked, how many actually bought. Lots of traffic plus low conversion means go investigate the price, photo, or description.
  3. Two pizzas each earned $300: Margherita (30 sold at $10) and Deluxe (6 sold at $50). Same kind of seller? Answer: No — one is cheap-and-many, one is pricey-and-few. AOV (average order value), the money per item, tells them apart.
  4. "Our pizza shop has 12,000 Instagram followers." Vanity or actionable? Answer: Vanity. It feels good but doesn't change any specific decision.
  5. "POOP: The Game has a 5x higher add-to-cart rate than our average game." Vanity or actionable? Answer: Actionable. It's a strong reason to spend more ad money on that product's page.
  6. Mike must choose which of 99 games to reprint before Nov 1. Which number should lead? Answer: Revenue plus units sold — what made money and what was popular — then layer AOV and conversion for the full picture.

The five KPIs: Revenue (total dollars earned), Units sold (how many sold), AOV (dollars per item), Conversion rate (of visitors, how many bought), Long-tail share (how concentrated revenue is across products). Rule: pick the number that answers the question, actionable over vanity.
