Security Threat Model

MedVault Patient Monitoring System

Client: Riverside General Hospital Prepared by: Junior Security Analyst Status: Draft, awaiting review
1

Executive Summary

Riverside General Hospital relies on MedVault patient monitoring devices to track vital signs across the ICU and general wards. These 50 devices continuously send heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen level data to nurses' stations and physician dashboards. When a patient's vitals fall outside safe ranges, the system triggers immediate alerts.

This threat model identifies security risks that could affect patient safety, data privacy, and hospital operations.

Good news: our review found no Critical risks in the MedVault deployment. The highest severity identified is High, and nothing requires immediate attention before the next audit cycle.

2

System Overview

  • 300-bed hospital (MedVault customer)
  • 50 MedVault patient monitoring devices (ICU and general wards)
  • Cloud-based data storage and analytics
  • Clinical dashboard for nurses and physicians
  • Real-time vital alerts with escalation protocols
  • Integration with Electronic Health Records (EHR)

Devices measure vitals every 5 seconds → hospital WiFi → local gateway → MedVault cloud (thresholds, alerts) → clinical dashboard and nurses' mobile devices.

Risk Summary

Top findings, sorted by priority, for the reader who won't read the full grid.

Critical

  1. Weak dashboard authentication (Spoofing), one stolen credential means full access to patient data and alert settings.
  2. Unencrypted device communications (Info Disclosure), vitals cross the hospital WiFi with no encryption; anyone on the network can capture them.

High

  1. Firmware tampering (Tampering), physical access allows firmware changes with no integrity check.
  2. Device network pivot (Elevation of Privilege), a compromised monitor becomes an entry point to the hospital network.
  3. Patient data export (Info Disclosure), bulk export allowed with no extra authorization.
