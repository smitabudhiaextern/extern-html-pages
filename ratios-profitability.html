Profitability

Does this business make money — and will the profits hold?

We're using NVIDIA's FY26 numbers (fiscal year ended January 2026) as the worked example through the four ratio families.

Why NVIDIA? You already know what NVIDIA does. It makes the GPUs that power gaming consoles, AI training, and most of the Claude / ChatGPT responses you've ever read. The product is concrete — you can picture it — which lets you focus on what the ratios mean instead of decoding the business.

Heads up: NVIDIA is a mega-cap (a very large company by market value, typically over $200B). Your Russell 2000 company won't look like this. The point isn't to match NVIDIA's numbers — it's to learn the framework on a business you already understand, then apply that framework to yours.

The Family · 5 Forecasting Ratios

The 5 profitability ratios you'll use when forecasting

These five ratios are what analysts assume, derive, or check when they project a company's future earnings. Click each card to flip and see what it tells you and how you'll use it in a forecast.

Gross Margin
(Revenue − COGS) / Revenue
What's left after the direct cost of making the product. Forecasting role: you assume this to derive COGS.
Operating Margin
Op Income / Revenue
What's left after running the business day-to-day. Forecasting role: you assume this to derive operating income.
EBITDA Margin
EBITDA / Revenue
What the business earns before non-cash items and financing. Forecasting role: you assume this to derive EBITDA — the input to most valuation multiples.
FCF Margin
(CFO − CapEx) / Revenue
The cash the business actually generates for shareholders. Forecasting role: the ultimate output of any value investing forecast.
ROIC
NOPAT / Invested Capital
The return per dollar tied up in operations. Forecasting role: sanity check on your forecast and input to DCF terminal value.

Framework · Judgment

How to know if a profitability number is good

Before we ask "is 71.1% gross margin good?", we need a framework. Every profitability ratio is judged the same three ways. Click each card to expand.

1

What's normal for this industry?

Click to expand
Anchor the number against peers. NVIDIA's 71.1% gross margin only means something when you compare it to other chipmakers (40–55%) and software companies (70–85%). Standard sources: Damodaran's NYU Stern industry data tables (free, sector-average margins), CSIMarket (data on comparable companies), and direct reads of peer 10-K filings.
2

What's driving the number?

Click to expand
Identify the source. For gross margin, the driver is COGS efficiency plus pricing power. For operating margin, it's how well the company controls operating costs as it scales. For EBITDA Margin, it's the underlying cash-generating ability. For ROIC, it's the operating return per dollar of capital actually invested. Knowing the driver tells you if the number is structural or fragile.
3

Will it hold?

Click to expand
Durability check. A high margin without a competitive advantage erodes the moment a competitor undercuts. A high ROIC with no reinvestment opportunities can't compound. Always ask: is there a reason this number will persist?

When you see "71% gross margin," run these three questions before deciding if it's good. The next slides are exactly that — you applying the framework to each ratio.

Ratio 2 · Operating Margin

Operating Margin: how it's calculated, judged, and used in a forecast

Calculate
WalkthroughOperating Margin = Operating Income / Revenue = 130,387 / 215,938 = 60.4%
Judge

Is 60.4% operating margin good for NVIDIA?

Right answer: Exceptional.
IndustryTypical op margin
Mature SaaS (Microsoft)~42%
NVIDIA60.4%
Other chipmakers (AMD, Intel)5–20%
Branded consumer15–25%
Industrials8–15%
Grocery / mass retail2–5%
A 60% operating margin means NVIDIA keeps roughly 60 cents of every dollar of revenue after paying for both the chips and all the R&D and sales costs. That's almost unheard of for any company selling physical products. It's the moat plus operating leverage — revenue grew 65% YoY while OpEx grew far slower.
📚 Where benchmarks come from: Damodaran's NYU Stern industry data tables · CSIMarket · Peer 10-Ks (pull 3–5 competitors' filings directly).
How you'll use Operating Margin in a forecast You can assume operating margin directly OR build it from gross margin minus OpEx as % of revenue. A lower OpEx-as-% of revenue means stronger operating leverage as the company scales — the analyst's signal that profits will grow faster than sales.

Ratio 3 · EBITDA Margin

EBITDA Margin: how it's calculated, judged, and used in a forecast

Calculate
WalkthroughEBITDA = Operating Income + D&A = 130,387 + 2,843 = $133,230M EBITDA Margin = EBITDA / Revenue = 133,230 / 215,938 = 61.7%

EBITDA = Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation & Amortization. Adds the non-cash D&A charge back to operating income, giving a rough proxy for cash-generating ability before financing and tax decisions.

Judge

Is 61.7% EBITDA Margin good for NVIDIA?

Right answer: Exceptional.
IndustryTypical EBITDA margin
Mature software30–40%
NVIDIA61.7%
Telecom / cable30–40%
Branded consumer18–25%
Mature industrials15–25%
BanksN/A
NVIDIA's EBITDA margin sits above mature software companies — on hardware revenue. The D&A add-back is small ($2.8B on $130B operating income) because NVIDIA's business model is asset-light: it designs chips and outsources manufacturing to TSMC, so its own depreciable assets are modest.
📚 Where benchmarks come from: Damodaran's NYU Stern industry data tables · CSIMarket · Peer 10-Ks (pull 3–5 competitors' filings directly).
How you'll use EBITDA Margin in a forecast Assume EBITDA margin to derive EBITDA, which is the input to EV/EBITDA multiples in valuation work. EBITDA Margin is the most commonly used profitability metric in M&A and credit analysis — bankers and lenders price deals off it.

Ratio 4 · FCF Margin

FCF Margin: how it's calculated, judged, and used in a forecast

Calculate
WalkthroughFree Cash Flow = CFO − CapEx = 102,718 − 6,042 = $96,676M FCF Margin = FCF / Revenue = 96,676 / 215,938 = 44.8%

CFO = Cash From Operations (the cash the business actually collected from running the business). CapEx = Capital Expenditures (cash spent on property, equipment, and intangible assets to keep the business running and growing).

Judge

Is 44.8% FCF Margin good for NVIDIA?

Right answer: Exceptional.
IndustryTypical FCF margin
Mature software25–35%
NVIDIA44.8%
Branded consumer8–15%
Mature industrials5–10%
Capital-intensive (telecom, utilities)0–10%, sometimes negative
Early-growth capex-heavyoften negative
NVIDIA generates 45 cents of free cash for every dollar of revenue — after paying for everything it needs to run and grow the business. Tiny CapEx ($6B) relative to massive operating cash flow ($103B). This is the number value investors care about most.
📚 Where benchmarks come from: Damodaran's NYU Stern industry data tables · CSIMarket · Peer 10-Ks (pull 3–5 competitors' filings directly).
How you'll use FCF Margin in a forecast This is the ultimate goal of value investing forecasting — what you actually want to know is how much cash the business will generate. Discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation uses projected FCF directly. Many value investors only look at FCF Margin and ignore accounting earnings entirely.

Ratio 5 · ROIC

ROIC: how it's calculated, judged, and used in a forecast

Calculate · click through each step
Step 1 · NOPATNOPAT = Operating Income × (1 − tax rate) = 130,387 × (1 − 15.1%) = 130,387 × 0.849 = $110,679M
Step 2 · Invested CapitalInvested Capital = Total Debt + Stockholders' Equity = 8,468 + 157,293 = $165,761M
Step 3 · ROICROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital = 110,679 / 165,761 = 66.8%

NOPAT = Net Operating Profit After Tax (what the business would earn from operations if it had no debt). Invested Capital = Debt + Equity (everything actually tied up in the business).

Judge

Is 66.8% ROIC good for NVIDIA?

Right answer: Exceptional. Typical "excellent" ROIC is 15%+. Anything above 25% is rare. 67% is in a class of its own.
IndustryTypical ROIC
Top-decile software20–30%
NVIDIA66.8%
Branded consumer10–20%
Industrials8–12%
Capital-intensive5–8%
A ROIC this high means each dollar reinvested in operations earns 67 cents back per year. That's the mathematical definition of a moat.
📚 Where benchmarks come from: Damodaran's NYU Stern industry data tables · CSIMarket · Peer 10-Ks (pull 3–5 competitors' filings directly).
How you'll use ROIC in a forecast You don't directly assume ROIC. Instead, you check it AFTER you've built the forecast — if your projected ROIC drifts away from the historical or peer average, your assumptions are probably off. Also used as the terminal multiple input in DCF (terminal value is often computed using ROIC × Invested Capital).

Recap · Bridge

Quick Recap

  • Five profitability ratios drive forecasts: Gross Margin, Operating Margin, EBITDA Margin, FCF Margin, ROIC.
  • All five margins come from the same income statement, walked down. Gross is the most optimistic; FCF Margin is what's actually left.
  • Three steps to judge any ratio: industry context, what's driving it, will it hold.
  • You'll ASSUME the four margins in your forecast and CHECK ROIC at the end as the sanity gate.
  • Diagnostic ratios (Net Margin, ROE, ROA, DuPont) come in the next module — they don't drive forecasts but explain WHY the numbers move the way they do.
