Three finance externships, and how a student picks one

All three put a student inside a real finance job, working on a real company, with a real expert as host. What changes is the question they answer about that company. Use this page to explain each one, show how they differ, and help a student choose.

What they are

Each externship answers one question about a company

Value Investing & Financial Modeling

Hosted by Yinan Zhao, senior research analyst at a value investment firm

“Is this stock worth buying, and at what price?”

  • You playAn equity research analyst on a small-cap value team
  • CompanyA small US public company the student picks
  • You makeA full financial model, a valuation, and an investment memo with a price target, pitched in 5 minutes
  • Time11 to 12 weeks, about 4 hours a week

FP&A & Private Company Valuation

Hosted by Attronica, with founder and CEO Niel Thakkar

“What is this business worth, and what should the founder do with it?”

  • You playA junior FP&A analyst at a company whose founder is preparing to sell
  • CompanyAttronica, a family-run IT and audio-visual company near Washington, D.C. Real audited books, shared under an NDA
  • You makeA division forecast, a DCF and comps, and a 4-page recommendation on the founder's options
  • Time8 weeks, about 3 to 4 hours a week, plus 3 live sessions with the CEO

Activist Investing

Hosted by Eric Su, founder of EHS Investments, a fund that takes stakes in small public companies

“Why is this company falling behind, and what should its board change?”

  • You playAn analyst at an activist investment fund
  • CompanyA lagging small US public company the student finds with a stock screen
  • You makeA 7-slide activist pitch deck, built up across the five projects
  • Time8 weeks, about 4 hours a week

How they do not differ

What all three share

The same in every one

  • Real companies, real documents. No made-up case studies. Students use public filings or a real company's audited books.
  • A real expert as host. Each one is built around someone who does this work for a living.
  • AI as a teammate, not the answer. Students use Claude and other AI tools to read, calculate and draft, and they check every number and make the final call themselves.
  • The same core skills. Reading financial statements, comparing a company with its peers, and backing a recommendation with evidence.
  • Remote and part-time. About 3 to 4 hours a week, alongside classes.
  • A recommendation at the end. Each ends with a recommendation made to a decision-maker. In Value Investing and Activist Investing, students can show the finished work in interviews. In FP&A, the NDA means they describe what they built but do not show it.

The two pairs students mix up

Value InvestingActivist Investing

Both look at small public companies the student picks. Value investing asks “is it cheap?” Activist investing asks “why is it cheap, and can the board fix it?” Value investing goes deep on the model. Activist investing skips the model and goes deep on evidence: the product, executive pay and shareholder votes.

They also work as a path. Activist Investing was designed to follow Value Investing, and a student can carry the same company into it if it passes the screen.

Value InvestingFP&A & Valuation

Both build a DCF and comps. Value investing sits on the buyer's side of a public stock. FP&A sits inside a private company, helping its founder weigh options before a sale. FP&A is shorter, and everyone works on the same company.

Helping a student choose

If a student says this, point them here

“I'm new to finance and a bit nervous.”Activist Investing. No finance background needed. FP&A is also open to beginners if they want spreadsheet work.
“I want to get really good at Excel models.”Value Investing & Financial Modeling. It has the most complete model of the three.
“I want to choose my own company.”Value Investing or Activist Investing. In FP&A, everyone works on Attronica.
“I want to see inside a real private company.”FP&A & Private Company Valuation. Students sign a real NDA and work with real books.
“I like research and arguing a point more than math.”Activist Investing. Students test the product, read filings and pitch a fix.
“I only have a couple of months.”FP&A or Activist Investing, both 8 weeks. Value Investing runs 11 to 12.
“I want to work in banking, private equity or consulting.”FP&A & Private Company Valuation. It mirrors how a business is valued for a sale.
“I want to pick stocks or work at a fund.”Value Investing first. Activist Investing makes a strong second externship.

Check before you publish

  • Attronica's numbers stay private. Students can name Attronica on a resume or LinkedIn, but their models and slides cannot be shared, and no real figures from the company can appear in marketing or student posts.
  • Do not quote Attronica's revenue or headcount. Our sources disagree, so leave size out or confirm it with Attronica first.
  • Do not criticize the company in Eric's sample campaign. Describe it only as his real, public campaign.
  • Value Investing is being redesigned. Length and the list of stocks students choose from may change, so keep copy to “a small public company you pick” and confirm the week count and weekly hours.
