How they do not differ

What all three share

The same in every one

  • Real companies, real documents. Students use public filings or a company's audited books.
  • A working expert as host. Each one is built around someone who does this work for a living.
  • AI as a teammate, not the answer. Students use Claude and other AI tools to read, calculate and draft, and they check every number and make the final call themselves.
  • The same core skills. Reading financial statements, comparing a company with its peers, and backing a recommendation with evidence.
  • Remote and part-time. A few hours a week, alongside classes.
  • A recommendation at the end. Each ends with a recommendation made to a decision-maker. In Value Investing and Activist Investing, students can show the finished work in interviews. In FP&A, the NDA means they describe what they built but do not show it.

The two pairs students mix up

Value InvestingActivist Investing

Same kind of company, different question. Value investing asks “is it cheap?” Activist investing asks “why is it cheap, and can the board fix it?”

Value InvestingFP&A & Valuation

Same valuation tools, different seat. Value investing sits on the buyer's side of a public stock. FP&A sits inside a private company.

Helping a student choose

If a student says this, point them here

“I'm new to finance and a bit nervous.”Activist Investing, or FP&A if they want spreadsheet work.
“I want to pick stocks or work at a fund.”Value Investing first, then Activist Investing. The two pair well, and a student can carry the same company into Activist Investing if it passes the screen.
