Slide 3 of 5 — Cash Flow Statement

The Cash Flow Statement answers a question the Income Statement can't: how much actual cash did the business generate, and where did it go? It starts from Net Income and adjusts for everything that's a non-cash item or a timing difference, then sorts the rest into three buckets — operating, investing, financing.

Slide 4 of 5 — How They Connect

The three statements aren't independent. Net Income is the bridge between them — it tells you what the business earned, it grows the equity sitting on the Balance Sheet, and it's the starting line on the Cash Flow Statement. Two flows tie the whole picture together.

Flow 1 — Net Income flows into Retained Earnings

Every dollar of Net Income lands in Retained Earnings, minus what NVIDIA paid out as dividends and buybacks. The roll-forward ties exactly.

Beginning Retained Earnings (Jan 26, 2025):   $68,038
+ Net Income:   $120,067

Flow 2 — Net Income starts the Cash Flow Statement

The Cash Flow Statement begins with the same Net Income, then adjusts for non-cash items and working capital changes to land at actual cash generated.

Net Income:   $120,067
+ Depreciation & amortization:   $2,843
+ Stock-based compensation:   $6,386
− Gains on investments:   ($8,918)
− Deferred income taxes:   ($1,424)
− Increase in accounts receivable:   ($15,399)
− Increase in inventories:   ($11,324)
+ Other working capital & non-cash items, net:   $10,487
= Net cash from operations:   $102,718 ✓
Net Income is the bridge between all three. It tells you what the business earned (Income Statement), it grows the equity sitting on the Balance Sheet (Retained Earnings), and it's the starting line for measuring actual cash generated (Cash Flow). One number, three views.
