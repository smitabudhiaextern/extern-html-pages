Design your columns — building fact_product_performance (text version)

A column earns its place in a summary table only if it answers a reorder question you will actually ask. Decide the columns one question at a time. For each question, pick the column that answers it.

Question 1 — Which games made the most money?

Correct answer: total_sales, the total revenue per game. Revenue is the number for "most money." Units sold answers a different question, and category already lives in dim_product. This question adds the total_sales column.

Question 2 — Are the games getting lots of ad clicks actually selling?

Correct answer: total_pageviews plus net_items_sold, traffic sitting next to sales. You need traffic and sales in the same row to spot a game with lots of clicks but few sales. This question adds the total_pageviews and net_items_sold columns.

Question 3 — You keep computing revenue divided by units for every game, every time. What is the move?

Correct answer: store it once as an aov column. A number you compute over and over gets stored once. For We're Doomed!: $3,810.47 divided by 104 equals $36.64. This question adds the aov column.

Question 4 — Should price go in your summary table?

Correct answer: No, because price changes over time, and a fact table holds settled numbers. The reject test is: if a value changes over time, leave it out. The same goes for anything already stored in dim_product, like category. This question adds no column. It teaches what to leave out.

The table you just built — fact_product_performance

Each question added a column. Together they form fact_product_performance, with columns game, units, revenue, aov, and pageviews:

  • We're Doomed!: 104 units, $3,810 revenue, $36.64 aov, 20,316 pageviews.
  • Dwellings of Eldervale – Oracle Hex Tile: 178 units, $1,270 revenue, $7.13 aov, 793 pageviews.
  • Trellis: 13 units, $328 revenue, $25.19 aov, 6,052 pageviews.
  • Master Thief: 18 units, $276 revenue, $15.34 aov, 639 pageviews.
