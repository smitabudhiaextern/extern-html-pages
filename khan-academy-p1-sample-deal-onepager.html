The deal · announced March 2014 · launched June 2015

The College Board opened its real test questions so Khan Academy could build SAT practice that was free for everyone.

One organisation writes the SAT. The other one had a learning platform that was already running and already free. The announcement came in March 2014, and the free official SAT practice launched in June 2015.

Announced March 2014 Launched June 2015
The trade · two parties

What each side gave, and why.

The scores tracked family income. In the class of 2014, students from families earning over $200,000 averaged 1722 out of 2400. Students from families earning under $20,000 averaged 1324, a gap of 398 points (NCES, from College Board data). Coaching that could close it cost around $1,000 a class, and private tutors more than $100 an hour (The Fiscal Times, 3 June 2015). So the test was partly measuring what a family could afford.

The College Board

Writes the SAT. Owns the question content.

What it gave

  • Official course content, so the practice came from the test maker rather than a guess at the test.
  • Early access to the new questions, before anyone selling prep.
  • The official designation: “official practice directly from the people who created the test.”

Why it said yes

On the record

“Assessment without opportunity is dead.”

David Coleman, the College Board. Said to the Texas Tribune, 16 March 2016.

Also on the record: the College Board approached Khan Academy, not the reverse.

My read

I think: going outside mattered as much as opening the content. Practice built by somebody else is easier to stand behind.

Nobody at the College Board has publicly said this. There are no quote marks on it because there is nothing to quote.

Khan Academy

Nonprofit. Runs a free learning platform.

What it gave

  • A working learning platform, already built and already running.
  • Engineering, the build work behind practice that adapts to the student using it.
  • Content production, building the lessons and then keeping them current as the test changed.
  • Free delivery to everyone, with no paywall and no trial.

Why it said yes

On the record

“Our mission at Khan Academy is a free, world-class education for anyone, anywhere, and college readiness is a crucial part of that.”

Sal Khan, Khan Academy. Said in the College Board’s own announcement, 2 June 2016. Quoted word for word.

Also on the record: credibility is the one uneven benefit Khan Academy names looking back. “we were a small organization, and it legitimized us even more,” Sal Khan, February 2026.

My read

I think: that credibility travelled further than the SAT work itself. Being the organisation the test maker chose reads as a reference before a first meeting.

Khan Academy has not publicly said this. There are no quote marks on it because there is nothing to quote.

The shape

What kind of deal this actually is.

Licensing

One side held content nobody else could make and opened it for the other side to build on. Neither organisation took over the other’s job: the questions stayed the College Board’s, and the platform, the build and the delivery stayed Khan Academy’s.

My call

The strongest thing in this deal is the thing neither side had to build: each one already owned exactly what the other one could not make, so the trade held without either of them changing what they were.
