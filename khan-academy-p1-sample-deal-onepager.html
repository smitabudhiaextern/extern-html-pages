The deal · announced March 2014 · launched June 2015

The College Board opened its real test questions so Khan Academy could build SAT practice that was free for everyone.

One organisation writes the SAT. The other one had a learning platform that was already running and already free. The announcement came in March 2014, and the free official SAT practice launched in June 2015.

The trade · two parties

What each side gave, what each side got, and why each said yes.

Read the two columns across, not down. What the College Board gave is the same list as what Khan Academy got, and the other way round. That is what makes it a trade rather than a favour.

gave on one side,

The College Board

Writes the SAT. Owns the question content.

What it gave The content, and the right to call the practice official

  • Official course content, so the practice came from the test maker rather than from a guess at the test.
  • Early access. Khan Academy saw the redesigned test before anyone selling prep did. TechCrunch, 5 March 2014, described the arrangement as rare and exclusive.
  • The official designation, so the practice could be described as coming from the organisation that writes the test. The College Board’s own page calls it “official practice directly from the people who created the test.” The College Board’s own page, quoted word for word.

What it got Khan Academy’s column, read across

  • A learning platform that already existed and already ran
  • The engineering behind practice that adapts to the student using it
  • The lessons themselves, and the work of keeping them current as the test changed
  • Free delivery to everyone, with no paywall and no trial

Why it said yes

On the record Quoted, with attribution

“Assessment without opportunity is dead.”

David Coleman, the College Board. Said to the Texas Tribune, 16 March 2016. Quoted word for word, and the words are his.

Also on the record: the College Board approached Khan Academy, not the reverse. Jeremy Singer ran the partner search that led to it.

My read My inference, not a quote

I think: going outside mattered as much as opening the content. Practice built by somebody else is easier to stand behind than practice the test maker grades itself on.

Nobody at the College Board has publicly said this. There are no quote marks on it because there is nothing to quote. It is my reading of what the deal does, and I could be wrong about it.

Khan Academy

Nonprofit. Runs a free learning platform.

What it gave The platform, the build, and the delivery

  • A working learning platform, already built and already running.
  • Engineering, the build work behind practice that adapts to the student using it.
  • Content production, building the lessons and then keeping them current as the test changed.
  • Free delivery to everyone, with no paywall and no trial.

What it got The College Board’s column, read across

  • Official course content from the organisation that writes the test
  • Early access to the redesigned test, ahead of anyone selling prep
  • The official designation for the practice it was building

Why it said yes

On the record Quoted, with attribution

“we were a small organization, and it legitimized us even more.”

Sal Khan, Khan Academy. Said on The Education Equation, the College Board’s podcast, February 2026, looking back on the partnership. Quoted word for word.

Also on the record: the reason Khan Academy gives publicly is mission, every time. Credibility is the one uneven benefit it names looking back.

My read My inference, not a quote

I think: that credibility travelled further than the SAT work itself. Being the organisation the test maker chose is the kind of thing other institutions read as a reference before they take a first meeting.

Khan Academy has not publicly said this. No quote marks, because there is nothing to quote. It is one person’s reading, mine, and it is a guess about what followed, not a fact about it.

The shape

What kind of deal this actually is.

Licensing

One side held content nobody else could make and opened it for the other side to build on. Neither organisation took over the other’s job: the questions stayed the College Board’s, and the platform, the build and the delivery stayed Khan Academy’s.

My call

The strongest thing in this deal is the thing neither side had to build: each one already owned exactly what the other one could not make, so the trade held without either of them changing what they were.
