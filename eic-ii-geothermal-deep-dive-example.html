Part 1: Context (the Primer)

A deep dive opens by defining its world, so any reader can follow. Here is how the Geothermal report set up its Context.

Geothermal: tapping heat stored in the Earth's crust. The core runs about as hot as the sun's surface, and that heat can be reached at different depths.

Permeability: the tiny gaps in rock that let water flow through it.

Hydrothermal: hot water circulating in naturally permeable rock, found about 1 to 3 km down, up to roughly 200°C.

Enhanced Geothermal (EGS): heat trapped in hot, dry rock that has no natural water or permeability, so engineers drill deep and create it. About 4 to 8 km down, above 200°C.

Notice: every specialized term is defined the moment it appears. That is exactly what your Context section does for your focus area.

Part 2: Industry impact (the oil-and-gas tie)

Then the report shows why the topic matters, and ties it to oil and gas with real numbers.

A real, growing market: geothermal has roughly 5x growth potential by 2050, with enhanced geothermal climbing from 0.4% to 8.5% of U.S. power.

It runs on oil and gas know-how: geothermal borrows the industry's own tools, directional and multilateral drilling plus downhole sensing that has cut exploration costs by about 35 to 55%. Oil-and-gas co-production is a direct synergy.

Cost per well drives everything: wells range from about $2.5M for shallow ones to about $25M for deep enhanced geothermal. That single number shapes whether a project makes sense.

Notice: it connects the topic to oil and gas and puts sourced numbers on why it matters. That is your Industry impact section.
