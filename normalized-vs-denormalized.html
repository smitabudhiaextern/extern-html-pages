Normalized vs Denormalized

Two ways of storing the same information. You've spent four Projects doing it one way. Your dashboard needs the other. Here's the difference, starting with a pizza shop.

The pizza shop, part 1

The kitchen keeps everything separate

Dough here, sauce there, cheese in the fridge, toppings in their own trays. Nothing is duplicated, and every ingredient sits in exactly one place. When an order comes in, someone assembles it from those parts.

That kitchen can make anything on the menu. But every single order costs a few minutes of assembly.

The pizza shop, part 2

The warmer holds slices that are already made

Someone already did the assembling. You point, they hand it over, five seconds. It isn't faster because anyone works quicker. It's faster because the work happened earlier.

The pizza shop, part 3

Two things are also true about that slice

1

It was made twenty minutes ago. If the kitchen changed the recipe since then, the slice under the warmer doesn't know about it.

2

Its price is now written in two places. There's a label on the warmer, and the same price is on the menu board behind the counter. Change one and forget the other, and the shop is quoting two different prices.

Hold on to both of those. They're the exact two costs you'll accept when you build your dashboard table.

Now in your database

The kitchen is a normalized database

That's what you've built across four Projects. Every fact lives in exactly one place. dim_product holds product details, shopify_sales holds sales figures, referrals holds traffic.

If a product name is wrong, there's one row to fix. And when you need facts from several tables at once, you JOIN them together, which is the assembly step.

This is why it's been the right design so far. You've been asking questions of the data, and questions change constantly.

Now in your database

The warmer is a denormalized table

That's what dashboard_metrics will be. You deliberately keep the same fact in more than one place, because it's faster to read that way. It will hold a copy of a revenue total that already exists in shopify_sales.

You aren't replacing the original or throwing anything away. You're keeping a pre-calculated copy that the dashboard can read without doing any work.

A dashboard isn't asking questions. It asks the same five questions every time, which is exactly when pre-making the answer pays off.

The trade you're making

The slice's two problems are your table's two problems

1

Your table is a snapshot. It's correct as of whenever you last rebuilt it, not as of right now.

2

The same revenue number now sits in two tables. Normally that's a mistake. Here it's the price of being fast.

For a dashboard, that trade is almost always worth making. Nobody minds a number being twenty minutes old. Everybody minds a page that takes four seconds to load.
