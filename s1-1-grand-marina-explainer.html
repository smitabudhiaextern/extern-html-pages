The Grand Marina is a giant hotel. 500 rooms, with water running everywhere all day: showers, the pool, the kitchen. When something goes wrong with the water here, it becomes a big, expensive problem fast. So the hotel keeps a close watch on it. Here is how that watch works.
🏨
The Grand Marina Hotel
500 rooms · water everywhere
Part 1 of 4
The Watchers (Water Sensors)
You can't put a person next to every pipe. So the hotel uses tiny devices called water sensors. Each one measures two simple things: how fast the water is flowing, and how hard it is pushing through the pipe. There is one in the main building, one at the pool, and one in the kitchen.
Part 2 of 4
The Postman (Message Hub)
The watchers can't talk to people directly. So every few seconds, each one sends its reading to a single spot, like a postman collecting the mail. This spot is the Message Hub. Every reading in the whole hotel passes through here.
Parts 3 and 4 of 4
The Logbook and The Lookout
From the hub, every reading goes two places. The Logbook (Record Keeper) saves it, so you can always look back at what happened. The Lookout (Operator Screen) shows it live on a screen a staff member watches, with a green light that means all is well.
The Whole System
Put It Together
That's the entire system. The water reaches the watchers, the watchers send readings to the postman, and the postman passes them to the logbook and the lookout. The readings flow one way: from the water, to the screen. In the next part, you'll meet the one thing that travels the other way.
One Last Thing
The Catch
Here is the important part. The screen only ever shows what the watchers say. So if a watcher is ever tricked into saying "all fine" when it isn't, the screen stays green while something is actually going wrong. And there are doorways into this network a stranger could use, like the hotel's own Guest Wi-Fi. That gap is what the rest of this project is about.
