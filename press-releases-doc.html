Press Releases — What to Watch For

Common reasons companies issue press releases beyond earnings — and what each one signals.

There are many reasons a company puts out a press release. These four cover the most common — and the ones that move the stock the most. Each has its own thing to read for.

  1. Leadership Changes
  2. Mergers & Acquisitions
  3. Divestitures & Business Sales
  4. Product Launches & Innovations
Type 1 of 4
Leadership Changes — What Does It Mean?

When a CEO, CFO, or board chair steps down, it can indicate:

  • A planned transition for a long-term strategy.
  • Performance-related issues leading to executive turnover.
  • Potential company restructuring.
What to Look For:
  • Is the departure sudden or planned?
  • Who is replacing the outgoing leader?
Example If a well-respected CEO resigns unexpectedly, stock prices might drop due to uncertainty. They can also rise when the new CEO is a strong candidate for the job — as was Disney's case due to Iger's previous stint as the CEO.

Mergers & Acquisitions — Growth or Red Flag?

When a company announces a merger or acquisition (M&A), it's a major event shared through a press release on its investor relations website.

Mergers occur when two companies combine to form a new entity, often to create synergies, reduce competition, or expand market reach. Acquisitions happen when one company buys another, sometimes to gain technology, talent, or customer base.

M&A can signal growth if the deal strengthens the company's competitive position, adds valuable assets, or fuels innovation. However, it can also be a red flag — a struggling company might acquire another to cover financial weaknesses, or an expensive deal could burden the buyer with debt.

What to Look For:
  • Does the acquisition align with the company's core strategy or distract from it?
  • How is the deal being financed — cash, debt, or stock?
  • Will the merger/acquisition lead to meaningful synergies (cost savings, new capabilities, expanded market access)?
  • How did the stock price react after the announcement?
Type 3 of 4
Divestitures & Business Sales — Streamlining Operations

Companies sometimes sell off parts of their business to focus on core operations or raise capital. This can be a strategic move to improve efficiency or an indicator of financial distress.

What to Look For:
  • Does the sale improve profitability and focus?
  • How do investors and analysts react? Check stock market trends after the press release.
  • Is the buyer gaining a competitive advantage through the acquisition?
Example — Alight's $1.2B Business Sale In 2024 Alight announced an agreement to sell its Payroll and Professional Services business for up to $1.2 billion. The next day, the stock closed at $10.32 on 8.1M+ shares of volume. Needham maintained a Buy rating and raised its price target from $12 to $13 — signaling confidence in the move.
Divestitures can be a positive (focus) or negative (distress) signal.

Product Launches & Innovations — Future Growth Potential

New product releases often lead to stock movements, especially for companies reliant on innovation.

What to Look For in the Press Release:
  • Competitive Edge: Does the product stand out from what competitors offer? Look for specifics — faster processing, unique AI capabilities, or other differentiators.
  • Market Reception: Are early customer reactions or pre-orders mentioned? Strong demand suggests the product could sell big.
Why it matters A product that opens a new revenue segment is read very differently from a refresh of an existing line. The press release is where the company signals which one this is — and how big the addressable market might be.
