Limitations & confounds — read before deciding

  • No internal baseline for Hydro & Attronica. Both launched in 2026 as narrative-native, so there is no before/after within the same program. Every Hydro/Attronica comparison is cross-program and therefore confounded by subject matter, instructor, and learner self-selection. Treat their results as directional, not causal.
  • The internal Yinan control is confounded by maturity, not just immaturity. The two narrative cohorts (#539 May 24, #562 Jun 21, n=262) are the newest Yinan cohorts; every standard-format control cohort started earlier (Jan–Apr). So it's a time-based before/after, and Yinan's own cohorts show a clear recency gradient — newer cohorts read lower on Project 1/completion simply because they've had less time. So narrative's lower Project 1 (0%, immature) and Orientation (Δ−6.1) are largely maturity artifacts, not format effects. A fair internal read needs either (a) #539/#562 to mature, or (b) a fixed-age metric ("% completing Project 1 within N days of registration") measured at equal cohort age — which this dashboard does not yet expose. Launch-date matching can't rescue this: there is no standard-format Yinan cohort from the same window.
  • The primary metric definition changed. The DDoc's gate was "Project 1 within 7 days of registration" (16%→32%). The dashboard now measures "Project 1 by the program's own deadline." These are not the same number, so results can't be held directly against the 16%/32% targets.
  • "P1 by deadline" is not comparable across programs. Each program sets its own due date; Hydro & Attronica never set one (inferred as start+8 days, a tight window). That's why their by-deadline rates read low. Cross-program reads lean on Orientation, raw Project 1, cancellation, and matured completion instead.
  • Controls differ by program. Hydro uses an enrollment-weighted pool of 4 technical peers (so no single program — e.g. Pfizer — distorts the benchmark); Attronica is compared only to Yinan's standard cohorts (closest finance match, your call); Yinan is a before/after vs its own past cohorts. The cross-program z-tests are valid two-sample tests but remain cross-program — a significant gap can still reflect subject matter or self-selection. Significant ≠ caused by the narrative format.
  • Completion is shown but not significance-tested. Matured-cohort completion uses a smaller, unstated denominator than enrollment, so a z-test on it would be unreliable — it's reported as a descriptive delta only.
  • Qualitative N is small and self-selected. ~12 substantive Hydro replies, ~2 Attronica, 0 Yinan so far. Responders skew engaged. Strong as direction, not proof.

Recommendation

Right now the case for narrative rests on two things — drop-off and sentiment — not on completion.

  1. Drop-off is the one metric that clearly improved. Every narrative cohort cancels less than its control: Attronica 20.6% vs Yinan 32.4% (−11.8 pts, p<0.001), Hydro 29.3% vs tech peers 34.7% (−5.4, p<0.001), Yinan narrative 21.0% vs its past cohorts 32.4% (−11.4). Caveat: newer cohorts have had less time to drop — part of this is maturity; confirm as they age.
  2. Qualitative sentiment is strongly positive: ~86% of substantive replies positive, 0% negative, ~14% mixed — and the mixed ones all say "keep it, just tighten the dialogue." No one called the story forced or inauthentic.
  3. The other metrics have not improved. Orientation is flat-to-slightly-lower, Project 1 reads lower (maturity-confounded), and completion is too early to call. This is not yet a completion-gains story.

Verdict: Conditional GO (DDoc "Mixed, lean positive"). Expand the pilot and adopt "narrative-lite" now. Standardize only once maturity-fair data confirms the drop-off gain and shows movement on completion.

Next steps

  1. Expand to the 3–5 externship pilot (DDoc positive branch), prioritizing programs where we can set a proper P1 deadline and, ideally, run a clean internal cohort split like Yinan.
  2. Adopt "narrative-lite" now: keep the protagonist and workplace framing, but tighten dialogue — short setup scene → clean technical steps. This is the single most-requested change and costs little.
  3. Fix the measurement before the next read: set explicit P1 due dates on narrative programs, and report against the DDoc's 7-day metric and the by-deadline metric so we're comparing like-for-like.
  4. Get a maturity-fair metric before the next read. Every narrative cohort (Yinan #539/#562, Attronica's 2 cohorts) is among the newest in its program, so current snapshot rates conflate format with cohort age. Ask the team for a fixed-age metric — "% completing Orientation / Project 1 within N days of registration" — measured at equal cohort age. That, not launch-date matching, is what makes the quant comparison valid.
  5. Re-read once the narrative cohorts mature (≈3+ weeks) and qualitative replies land.
  6. Decision gate: proceed to standardization only if, on maturity-matched data, narrative shows a non-trivial lift on at least two of {fixed-age P1, lifetime P1 completion, cancellation} against both internal and peer controls.
