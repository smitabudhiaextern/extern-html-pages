The Reasons People Actually Abandon

Not Every Abandoned Cart Has the Same Story

91% abandonment sounds like one problem. It's actually six different ones, each with its own signature in the data. Here's what each looks like, and how you'd go looking for it.

Hypothesis 1 of 6

Sticker Shock at Checkout

The Scenario

You add a $19.99 board game to your cart. At checkout, shipping adds $14 more. You close the tab.

How You'd Test It

Compare subtotal to total on abandoned carts. If shipping eats up a big share of the total, that's the signature.

Hypothesis 2 of 6

Just Pricing It Out

The Scenario

You open the same board game in three tabs across three stores, just to see who's cheapest. None of those carts were ever going to become a sale.

How You'd Test It

Look for carts with low quantity and a short time between creation and abandonment. Quick in, quick out.

Hypothesis 3 of 6

Forced to Make an Account

The Scenario

Checkout demands you create an account and verify your email before you're even allowed to pay. You close the tab and buy the same game somewhere that lets you check out as a guest.

How You'd Test It

Hard to see directly in this data; it would need session-level timing we don't have. Worth keeping on the list even without a clean test.

Hypothesis 4 of 6

Not a Real Shopper at All

The Scenario

A cart adds 47 units of the same $60 game at 3 AM. No real customer buys 47 copies of one board game in the middle of the night.

How You'd Test It

Suspicious quantity patterns, the same email creating many carts, and off-hours timing are the tells.

Hypothesis 5 of 6

Shipping Doesn't Reach Them

The Scenario

You're shopping from a country Breaking Games doesn't actually ship to. You don't find out until checkout, when there's suddenly no way to finish.

How You'd Test It

Look at billing and shipping country on abandoned carts for places outside the store's normal shipping zones.

Hypothesis 6 of 6

It Was Never Actually Available

The Scenario

You add it to your cart, then find out at checkout it's out of stock. That's not really abandonment. It's an inventory problem wearing an abandonment costume.

How You'd Test It

This would show up as certain products getting abandoned at a much higher rate than others.
