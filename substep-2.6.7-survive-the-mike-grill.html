Mike's three questions — defending your numbers (text version)

Mike is about to commit real money to the reorder. He asks three questions. For each, the answer that earns his trust is given.

1. Where did this number come from?

Best answer: "About $13.3K, from the total_sales column. Here's the saved query." You can name the source and reproduce it, so Mike trusts it. Saying "somewhere in the sales data" or quoting false precision like "$13,323.56" both fail.

2. What if this estimate is off?

Best answer: "Revenue is exact. The pageview number is an estimate, but this game sits well below the reorder line either way, so the call holds." You name which numbers are soft and show the decision survives. Claiming no margin, or not knowing the margin, both fail.

3. What would change your mind?

Best answer: "If we had data on which ad each buyer actually clicked, two campaigns might move from cut to keep." You know the limit of your data and what you would build next. "Nothing, I'm confident" or "I'd start over" both fail.

Takeaway: name the source, flag the estimates, and know what would change your mind. Keep those three answers ready before every real conversation.
