What Your Two Charts
Will Look Like

These are the two charts the dashboard template is built for. Look at the shape of each one and what it makes obvious at a glance, then decide whether they are the right two for your story.

Chart 1

Revenue concentration, as a doughnut

A doughnut chart answers one question well, how much of the whole does one slice take. Here it shows that the five best-selling products earn most of the revenue on their own.

Use a doughnut when the story is about share of a whole. It is a poor choice for more than about four segments, because thin slices become impossible to compare.

Chart 2

Traffic by channel, as bars

A bar chart is for comparing separate categories against each other. Here it shows where the site's visitors actually come from.

Your own bars may differ. The channel groupings come from the dim_referrer table you built in Project 3, so how many channels you show, and what counts as one, depends on the taxonomy you chose. Direct, Paid Social and Organic Search are the three that dominate regardless.

Your call

These two are the defaults, not the only option

The two defaults are a doughnut for revenue concentration and a bar chart for channel traffic. They work because the first is a share-of-whole story and the second is a comparison story.

If a different chart would tell your Q4 story better, for example comparing raw abandonment against the honest figure you calculated, use that instead and say why you swapped it.

Pick the chart that matches the shape of your finding. A share of a whole wants a doughnut. A comparison between categories wants bars.
