Stop 1 of 8 · The total

SUM — add a range

Linden reports Total Revenue on its 10-K. Before you trust it, you'll verify it from the segments — adding the three rows yourself.

How would you do that? Which Excel formula could you use?

The formula: =SUM(range).
Your sheet so far Linden
Teach

SUM — add a range

Apply it.
  1. Find the range you want to add — a continuous block of cells (rows or columns).
  2. Click an empty cell where the total will live.
  3. Type the formula. Start with =SUM(, then either drag-select your range or type cell references like B2:B4, and close with ).
  4. Hit Enter. The cell shows the sum.
  5. Tip: check the range every time. Long lists are where =SUM(B2:B10) quietly misses rows 11–12.
Tiny example. =SUM(A1:A3) where A1=10, A2=20, A3=30 returns 60.
Your question

What's Linden's 2021 Total Revenue?

Your three segments are in column B (2021): Legacy 800, Cloud 0, Services 200. You want to verify Linden's 2021 Total Revenue. Type the SUM formula into cell B5 of your sheet. What does it return?

Need help? Ask an AI assistant

Copy this prompt into Gemini, Claude, or ChatGPT:

I have three numbers in cells B2, B3, B4 of a Google Sheet — they are 800, 0, and 200. What formula gives me their total in cell B5? Just give me the formula and the answer.
Correct — 1,000

SUM grabs B2, B3, B4 (800 + 0 + 200) and returns the total.

=SUM(B2:B4) // returns 1,000

Drag the formula across columns and you get 950, 1,000, 1,050 — matching the totals you'll need for the P&L.

Why it matters. The most common SUM error in financial models isn't the math — it's the range. You SUM B2:B10 when the segments live in B2:B12, and the last two rows quietly vanish. Check the range every time. The senior reviewing your model will.

Stop 2 of 8 · Two-cell math

Subtraction, division, and growth rate

Legacy Software fell from $800M in 2021 to $100M in 2024. You need to convert that drop into a percentage growth rate — the metric every investor reads first.

How would you compute it? Which Excel formula could you use?

The formula: =end / start − 1 (then format the cell as %).
Your sheet so far Linden
Teach

Two-cell math

Apply it.
  1. Pick your two cells. One is your starting value, one is your ending value. For a 4-year growth, starting = the first year, ending = the last year.
  2. Click an empty cell where the answer will live (the question below tells you which — for this stop, F2).
  3. Type the formula. Start with =, then [ending cell] / [starting cell] - 1. Use your real cell references — for example, if starting is in B2 and ending in E2, you'd type =E2/B2-1.
  4. Hit Enter. You'll see a number like 0.125 or -0.875. That's the math — but not yet the format an analyst wants.
  5. Format the cell as a percentage. Click the cell, then any of these:
    • Press Ctrl + Shift + 5 (Mac: ⌘ + Shift + 5)
    • Click the % button on the toolbar
    • Or use Format → Number → Percent in the menu
    Now the cell reads -87.5% instead of -0.875. Negative is fine — it just means the value shrank.
Tiny example. A12 holds $120 (this year's revenue), A11 holds $100 (last year's). =A12/A11-1 returns 0.20. Why the −1? Dividing end by start gives you a ratio like 1.20 — meaning "you have 120% of where you started." Subtract 1 to strip out the original 100% you started with, and you're left with just the change: +20%. Format as percentage → +20%.
Your question

How badly has Legacy collapsed?

You want to know how badly Legacy Software has declined from 2021 to 2024 — as a growth rate. Legacy was 800 in B2, 100 in E2. Type the growth-rate formula in cell F2 and format the cell as a percentage. What does it display?

Copy this prompt into Gemini, Claude, or ChatGPT:

I have a value of 800 in cell B2 and 100 in cell E2 in Google Sheets. What formula gives me the growth rate from 2021 (B2) to 2024 (E2)? Format the answer as a percentage.
Correct — −87.5%

=E2/B2-1 // returns -0.875 → -87.5%

Legacy's revenue has collapsed by nearly nine-tenths in four years. This is the formula running underneath every "% growth" claim in every pitch deck.

Why it matters. Drag the formula and let references shift, and you're now dividing Q2 gross profit by Q3 revenue. The number still calculates. Nothing tells you it's wrong.

The formula: =base * rate.

Teach

Multiplication — rate × base

Apply it.
  1. Identify your two cells — one holds a base value, the other holds a rate or driver.
  2. Click an empty cell for the result.
  3. Type = then the base cell reference (e.g., A1), then *, then the rate cell (e.g., B1).
  4. Hit Enter. The cell shows base × rate.
  5. Reminder: rates entered as 0.15 mean 15%. If you typed 15, you'd get 15× the base (very wrong).
Tiny example. =100*0.21 returns 21.
Your question

What's Linden's 2024 Tax Expense?

Pre-Tax Income for 2024 is in cell E7 (=270), and the tax rate is in cell G6 (=21%, stored as 0.21). Type the formula in cell E8. What does it return?

Need help? Ask an AI assistant

Copy this prompt into Gemini, Claude, or ChatGPT:

In Google Sheets, cell E7 holds 270 and cell G6 holds 21% (= 0.21). What formula in cell E8 gives me 270 × 21%?
Correct — $56.7M

270 × 0.21 = 56.7. That's exactly the tax expense for 2024.

=E7*G6 // returns 56.7

Why it matters. The full forecast is built on multiplications like this — base in one cell, assumption in another, product is your forecast line. Wrong assumption cell, wrong forecast row, and the error compounds for every year you project forward.

Stop 4 of 8 · Conditional logic

IF — run a test, return a value

You want a Flag column that labels each segment Growing or Shrinking based on its growth rate — so an analyst eye can scan and read the story instantly.

How would you build that flag? Which Excel formula could you use?

The formula: =IF(test, value_if_true, value_if_false).
Your sheet so far Linden
Teach

IF — branch on a test

Apply it.
  1. Decide your test — a condition that's either true or false (e.g., A1>0).
  2. Decide your two return values — text in quotes ("Yes") or numbers, one for each outcome.
  3. Click an empty cell.
  4. Type =IF(test, value_if_true, value_if_false). Example: =IF(A1>0, "Yes", "No").
  5. Hit Enter. The cell returns one of your two values.
Tiny example. =IF(5>0, "Yes", "No") returns "Yes".
Your question

Flag each segment "Growing" or "Shrinking"

You want a column that flags each segment based on its growth rate. Type the IF formula in cell H2 using F2 (Legacy's growth rate) as the test cell. Legacy's growth rate is −87.5% (F2 = −0.875). What does H2 return?

Need help? Ask an AI assistant

Copy this prompt into Gemini, Claude, or ChatGPT:

In Google Sheets, cell F2 holds -0.875 (a negative growth rate). What formula in cell H2 returns the text "Growing" if F2 is positive, or "Shrinking" if F2 is negative?
Correct — "Shrinking"

−0.875 is not greater than 0, so the test fails and IF returns the second value.

=IF(F2>0, "Growing", "Shrinking") // returns "Shrinking"

Apply the formula to all three segments: Legacy → Shrinking, Cloud → Growing (we'll handle Cloud's special case in Stop 6), Services → Growing.

Why it matters. IF is the foundation of every conditional in a model — covenant breached or not, margin above threshold or below, segment grew or declined. You'll wrap it around growth rates, ratios, dates, and string matches for the rest of your career.

Stop 5 of 8 · Lock the reference

Absolute reference — the dollar signs

You already have 2024 tax expense. Now you need 2021, 2022, 2023 — each year's Pre-Tax Income times the same 21%. You want to drag one formula across with the rate locked in place.

How do you lock it? What's the Excel trick?

The trick: lock the rate cell with dollar signs, e.g., $G$6.
Your sheet so far Linden
Teach

Lock a reference with $ signs

Apply it.
  1. Identify which input should NOT move when you drag — usually a single cell holding a rate, FX rate, share count, or constant.
  2. Type the formula in the first cell. Wrap the locked input in dollar signs (e.g., $B$1 locks both column and row).
  3. Quick shortcut: put your cursor on the cell reference inside the formula and press F4 (Windows) or Cmd+T (Mac). Each press cycles $B$1 → B$1 → $B1 → B1.
  4. Hit Enter.
  5. Now drag the formula across (or down). The locked reference stays put; the unlocked ones shift.
Tiny example. Suppose A2 holds revenue and F1 holds a growth assumption. =A2*$F$1 — drag it down and A2 becomes A3, A4 (good, the row moves), but F1 stays F1 (good, the assumption doesn't drift).
Your question

Fill the rest of the Tax Expense row

You already have 2024 tax expense in E8 (from Stop 3). Now you want to fill in B8, C8, D8 — each year's Pre-Tax Income (row 7) multiplied by the same 21% in G6. You'll type the formula in B8 and drag it right. Which formula works?

Need help? Ask an AI assistant

Copy this prompt into Gemini, Claude, or ChatGPT:

In Google Sheets, I have Pre-Tax Income in row 7 (cells B7 through E7) and a tax rate of 21% in cell G6. I want to type a formula in B8 that gives me B7 × 21%, then drag it right to C8, D8, E8 — with the tax rate locked in G6. What formula?
Correct — =B7*$G$6

Drag it right: B7 becomes C7, D7, E7 (year moves correctly), $G$6 stays put. Every year's pre-tax income gets multiplied by the same 21% rate.

=B7*$G$6 // B8=52.5, C8=44.1, D8=50.4, E8=56.7

E8 matches what you computed in Stop 3 — the row ties out.

Why it matters. This is the formula behind the question seniors ask more than any other: "Where did this number come from?" Drag a tax rate down a column without locking it, and by row 30 your formula is multiplying by an empty cell. The model returns zero. Everything still calculates. The senior notices.

Stop 6 of 8 · Handle errors cleanly

IFERROR — wrap a formula

Cloud Platform was $0 in 2021 and $150M in 2022 — but your growth formula breaks with #DIV/0! because you can't divide by zero. You need to handle the error cleanly so the cell shows something useful instead.

How would you wrap it? Which Excel formula could you use?

The wrap: =IFERROR(formula, fallback).
Your sheet so far Linden
Teach

IFERROR — catch errors gracefully

Apply it.
  1. Write your normal formula first — see if it errors (#DIV/0!, #N/A, #REF!).
  2. If it does, wrap it: =IFERROR(your_formula, "fallback").
  3. The fallback can be text ("n/a", "new"), a number (0), or another formula.
  4. Hit Enter. The cell now shows your fallback whenever the inner formula errors.
  5. Use it on ratios where the denominator could be zero, or lookups that might fail.
Tiny example. =IFERROR(10/0, "n/a") returns "n/a" instead of #DIV/0!.
Your question

Fix Cloud's broken growth cell

Cloud Platform was 0 in 2021 (B3). You tried =E3/B3-1 in F3, but B3 is 0 so the cell shows #DIV/0!. You want the cell to show "new" instead. What formula in F3 fixes it?

Need help? Ask an AI assistant

Copy this prompt into Gemini, Claude, or ChatGPT:

In Google Sheets, my formula =E3/B3-1 returns #DIV/0! because B3 is zero. How do I wrap it so the cell shows "new" instead of an error?
Correct — =IFERROR(E3/B3-1, "new")

The cell now shows "new" instead of #DIV/0!. A reviewer can tell at a glance which segments are brand new vs. just declining.

=IFERROR(E3/B3-1, "new") // returns "new"

Why it matters. When you ship a model with #DIV/0! showing in any cell, that's the first thing a reviewer sees. Not your insight. Not your work. The error.

Stop 7 of 8 · Pull a value across tabs

VLOOKUP (and why you'll graduate to INDEX-MATCH)

Imagine Linden's segment data lives on a separate Segments tab and you're building the IS on a different tab. You need to pull Cloud's 2024 revenue across the gap.

How would you fetch a value from another tab? Which Excel formula could you use?

The formula: =VLOOKUP(lookup, table_range, col_number, FALSE).
Your sheet so far Linden
Lookup source Segments
Teach

Two formulas, same job

Apply it.
  1. Identify what you're looking up — a name or key value.
  2. Identify the table range — must START with the column containing the lookup key.
  3. Count the column number — your answer column, counted from the start of the range. (Key in column A, answer in column C → column number 3.)
  4. Type =VLOOKUP(lookup, table_range, col_number, FALSE). The FALSE forces an exact match (you almost always want this in finance models).
  5. If your table is on a different tab, prefix the range with the tab name: Segments!A:E.
Tiny example. =VLOOKUP("Cloud Platform", A:E, 5, FALSE) finds "Cloud Platform" in column A and returns the value from the 5th column in the same row.
Your question

Pull Cloud's 2024 revenue across tabs

You want to pull Cloud Platform's 2024 revenue from the Segments tab into a cell in your main Linden tab. Pick any empty cell in Linden (say, J1). Type a VLOOKUP that looks up "Cloud Platform" on the Segments tab and returns the value from the 5th column. What does it return?

Need help? Ask an AI assistant

Copy this prompt into Gemini, Claude, or ChatGPT:

In Google Sheets, I have a tab called "Segments" with segment names in column A and 2021–2024 revenue in columns B–E. From a different tab called "Linden", what VLOOKUP formula pulls Cloud Platform's 2024 revenue into a cell?
Correct — 700

VLOOKUP found "Cloud Platform" in column A of the Segments tab and returned the value in the 5th column (E) — which is 2024 revenue.

=VLOOKUP("Cloud Platform", Segments!A:E, 5, FALSE) // returns 700

Why analysts prefer INDEX-MATCH. A coworker inserts a "2025 Estimate" column between 2024 and the next year. VLOOKUP still points to "the 5th column" — but the 5th column is now 2025, not 2024. The formula silently returns the wrong year. INDEX-MATCH references column E directly, so it survives the insert.

Why it matters. VLOOKUP is the entry point. INDEX-MATCH is what gets used in production models because it doesn't quietly break when the source data moves around.

Stop 8 of 8 · Conditional sum

SUMIF — sum only when

You have a Growing/Shrinking flag column from Stop 4. Now you want the 2024 revenue total of only the Growing segments — the "good" side of the business in one number.

How would you sum only the matching rows? Which Excel formula could you use?

The formula: =SUMIF(criteria_range, criteria, sum_range).
Your sheet so far Linden
Teach

SUMIF — sum when a condition is met

Apply it.
  1. Identify your criteria range — the column with labels you want to filter on.
  2. Identify your criteria — the label you want to match (in quotes if text: "Yes").
  3. Identify your sum range — the column with values to add (only matching rows are added).
  4. Type =SUMIF(criteria_range, criteria, sum_range). Example: =SUMIF(A:A, "Yes", B:B).
  5. Hit Enter. The cell shows the sum of only matching rows.
Tiny example. =SUMIF(A:A, "Yes", B:B) sums column B only for rows where column A says "Yes".
Your question

Total revenue of the Growing segments

Column H has each segment's Growing/Shrinking flag. Column E has 2024 revenue. You want the total 2024 revenue of only the Growing segments. Type a SUMIF formula in any empty cell (say J3). What does it return?

Need help? Ask an AI assistant

Copy this prompt into Gemini, Claude, or ChatGPT:

In Google Sheets, column H has the text "Growing" or "Shrinking" for each row, and column E has revenue numbers. What formula sums column E only for rows where column H is "Growing"?
Correct — $950M

SUMIF added 2024 revenue only for rows where the flag was "Growing": Cloud (700) + Services (250). Legacy (Shrinking, 100) was skipped.

=SUMIF(H2:H4, "Growing", E2:E4) // returns 950

The Growing portion of Linden's business is $950M of the $1,050M total. Legacy is now only 9.5% of the company.

Why it matters. SUMIF is the clean way to handle messy data — leave it raw, write the formula that asks the question. You'll use it on segment data, geographic splits, customer cohorts, and any roll-up where the labels live next to the numbers.

SUM

IF

Use it when: you want a flag column (Growing/Shrinking, Pass/Fail, etc.).

Absolute Reference

$G$6

Use it when: you're dragging a formula and one input (rate, FX, share count) shouldn't move.

IFERROR
