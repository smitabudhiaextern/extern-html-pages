Before you start

Four chains, keep or kill

Four finished pieces of arithmetic, each one somebody's estimate of what a deal could be worth. You read a chain, then you commit: keep it, meaning you would put it in front of somebody, or kill it, meaning it has to come out. Nothing is explained until after you have chosen. Each chain either holds up or does not, and how many of each is not stated anywhere, so judge every one on its own.

Input. A number you took from a source instead of choosing it yourself. Every input on these four screens names where it came from and the period it covers.

Assumption. A number you chose because no source hands it to you. It can be a sensible choice and it is still a choice.

Derived. The result of your own arithmetic rather than a figure a source reports. You already carry this word, and it goes on every answer you work out yourself.

Illustrative. A figure put on screen to give a sense of scale. It is not a forecast and it is not a promise about what will happen.

The test, in one line. Two sourced inputs, one operation, the answer labelled derived, and one sentence saying what the number does not claim. Every chain is measured against that and nothing else.

Every figure on the four screens carries a link you can open yourself. Keys 1 for keep and 2 for kill work on every chain.

Where the four chains landed

Same test every time. Two inputs, one operation, and the question of whether the two inputs are allowed to be next to each other.

The plainest one is the one that held. Chain 3 is a single multiplication with two figures read off one page on one day, and it is the only one of the four you could take into a room. Chain 4 had six rows, a running total and an answer to the nearest ten thousand dollars, and it is worth the least of the four. Rows are not evidence, and a table is not a source.

You have run this shape once already. Earlier in the program you divided Coursera's Enterprise segment revenue of $255.3 million by its 1,730 paying enterprise customers, both figures for the year ended 31 December 2025, and you labelled the answer derived (Coursera, Form 10-K for the year ended 31 December 2025). One source, one period, one operation. That is the chain that survives here, with a different name on it.

That is the end of this one. Nothing further is required.
