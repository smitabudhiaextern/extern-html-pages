Defending Your Assumptions

The five defenses every assumption needs.

Every number in your model needs to be defended on at least one of these five grounds. Strong assumptions usually stand on two or three.

Defense 1 of 5

Industry Benchmark

What it is

Your assumption matches what comparable peers run. You check it against published sector data, peer 10-Ks, or competitor disclosures.

When it works best

When your peer set is genuinely comparable in business model, scale, and geography.

Example

You assume 38% gross margin for a mid-size software business. Damodaran's NYU Stern sector tables show the software sector running 35–42%. Your number sits inside the range.

The trap

Peers look comparable on the surface but operate a structurally different business. Your defense collapses on inspection.

Defense 2 of 5

Historical Trend

What it is

The metric has been moving in this direction long enough that continuing the line is the defensible call. You're extending a real pattern, not guessing.

When it works best

When the metric is stable and no structural shift is visible in the recent reporting.

Example

Gross margin has expanded 50 basis points (1 bps = 0.01%; 50 bps = 0.5%) per year for three straight years on the back of a steady pricing program. Assuming another +50 bps next year is defensible.

The trap

A structural shift is already underway and the line breaks. New competitor, input-cost spike, or a product cycle ending.

Defense 3 of 5

Management Guidance

What it is

The company has publicly committed to a number — on an earnings call, at an investor day, or in the annual letter.

When it works best

When guidance is specific, recent, and from a management team with a track record of hitting it.

Example

On the most recent earnings call the CFO said the company is targeting 25% operating margin by 2027. You build to that path and cite the call.

The trap

Companies miss their own targets often. Guidance is a data point, not a fact.

Defense 4 of 5

Macro / Cycle Context

What it is

Your assumption fits where we are in the business cycle. Rates, inflation, recession risk, or an industry-specific cycle.

When it works best

For cyclical businesses where the cycle is the dominant variable.

Example

For a homebuilder, you assume revenue growth softens next year because the rate environment is cooling demand. The cycle backs the call.

The trap

You over-index on one macro variable and miss the company-specific story sitting on top of it.

Defense 5 of 5

Your Specific Thesis

What it is

You have a real business case the other four defenses miss. A new automation initiative, a pricing reposition, a leadership change driving execution.

When it works best

When you can name the specific lever, point to where it shows up in the filings, and explain the timing.

Example

You assume SG&A drops 200 bps over two years because the company announced a back-office automation rollout in its last 10-K and the savings start hitting in the second half.

The trap

The "thesis" is vibes — a feeling about the company with no specific lever behind it.
