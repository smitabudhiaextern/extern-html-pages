What a school gets without paying, and what paying adds

Khan Academy's lessons are free to anybody with a web browser, and an earlier module already told you that. What you have not done is put the free version and the paid version next to each other and read down both.

Seven screens for the comparison: the free side, then four things paying adds, then the two ways a school buys it. After that, three short situations where you make the call yourself and commit to an answer before anything is revealed.

The free side, in one card

No account, no school, no payment, no country test. Anybody with a browser opens a course and starts work. The whole library of lessons, videos and practice sits behind no door at all. That is the version nearly everybody using Khan Academy is on.

Now the four things paying adds, one screen each.

The first thing paying adds: a screen for the teacher

On the free version a teacher is a visitor like anybody else. On a paid version she logs in to a screen built for running a class, and two different things arrive on it.

One is work already done for her. Khan Academy reads what the class has been doing and hands her a short list of things worth acting on, each with a button that acts on it.

The other is not from Khan Academy at all. It is from the office that bought the software. The district sets the goals, and the goals turn up on the teacher's own screen.

The third thing paying adds: somebody else sets the accounts up

Rostering means loading the list of who is in which class into the software, so the accounts exist before anybody has touched them.

The two paid arrangements do it differently, and the pricing page gives each of them one line. The larger arrangement gets "Automated, seamless rostering through Clever or ClassLink with prioritized technical support". The smaller one gets "Teacher-directed rostering".

Clever and ClassLink are separate companies that hold a district's list of pupils and classes and pass it to software the district has bought. So on the larger arrangement the accounts build themselves out of the district's own records. On the smaller one a teacher builds the class by hand.

This is the difference that decides what a student notices, which is why it is on its own screen. It is also the one with nothing to show you.

The two ways to buy it

Two arrangements, and the line between them is a count of students. One publishes a price. The other does not.

Khan Academy publishes a price for one district tier and not the other, khanacademy.org/schools/pricing, captured 8 September 2026. A real page, captured on the day.

Enterprise. More than 1,000 student licenses. No published price at all. The button asks you to request a free demo, which means the price comes out of a conversation.

Enterprise Starter. 1,000 or fewer. $10 per student per year. The button says Buy Now, so this one is bought online.

Both cards carry the same line, in Khan Academy's own words: "For U.S. schools and districts seeking grade-level success for all students", so this is a United States arrangement. And one caution: that is a live commercial page, and prices on live commercial pages change without telling anybody. $10 per student per year is what the page said on 8 September 2026, the day it was read.

Sometimes you can tell. Often you cannot.

Two of those three came back "cannot tell", and that was the right answer both times. It is not a way of dodging the question. A person who wrote it down had finished the job correctly. The one in the middle is different: there the evidence does point, and reaching for the unknown walks past something real.

Only one of the things paying adds is visible from where you are standing, and it is not a feature. It is who did the setting up. Everything else is a screen an adult logs in to, or a service delivered to adults away from the classroom, and none of that shows from outside the building.

So the skill is not knowing the features. It is knowing which of the two situations you are in: the one where the evidence in front of you actually points, and the one where the honest answer is that you cannot tell yet. No new facts on this screen.
