A 2-minute primer

How a spreadsheet is organized

Brand new to spreadsheets? This quick visual primer walks you through the four parts of every sheet — cells, the formula bar, the difference between typed numbers and formulas, and the anatomy you'll see in Excel, Google Sheets, or any tool.

Four short stops, each with a small animated demo. By the end, you'll be ready for the formulas walkthrough.

Stop 1 of 4 · The grid

Cells and addresses

A spreadsheet is a grid. Every box is a cell, identified by its column letter and row number — B4 means column B, row 4.

That's A1 notation, the address every formula uses.

Stop 2 of 4 · Behind the cell

The formula bar

Look at the top of the sheet. The formula bar shows what's actually inside the selected cell.

Sometimes it's a typed number. Sometimes it's a formula like =A1*2 — the cell shows the result, the formula bar shows the math.

Stop 3 of 4 · Inputs vs math

Typed numbers vs formulas

Cells hold either a hardcoded value (someone typed 47.3 and that's what it is) or a formula (a calculation that updates when its inputs change).

Analysts color-code these — typed inputs in blue, formula cells in black. So you can scan a model and see where the math lives.
