A model has structure

How the three statements connect

Every financial model is built on three statements: Income Statement, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow. They aren't independent — they link in specific places.

If you understand those links, the model stops feeling like a maze and starts feeling like a circuit.

What you'll see. Linden Holdings' three statements, side by side. Then we walk through the four linkages that hold every 3-statement model together: Net Income → Retained Earnings, Net Income → Cash Flow, Ending Cash → Balance Sheet Cash, and CapEx → PP&E.

Stop 1 · The three statements

Meet the three statements

Before we look at the linkages, meet the three statements that every model is built on.

IS

Income Statement

What happened over a period. Revenue, costs, profit.

BS

Balance Sheet

What the company has and owes at a single moment. Assets, Liabilities, Equity.

CF

Cash Flow Statement

How cash actually moved over a period.

Each statement asks a different question. The model is how they answer each other.

Stop 3 · Linkage 1 of 4

Net Income → Retained Earnings

How it links

The Income Statement ends with Net Income. That number doesn't just disappear — it lands in two places. Watch the first: Net Income for the period gets added to Retained Earnings on the Balance Sheet.

When you read a Balance Sheet year-over-year, RE is the running tally of every year's profit (minus dividends).

Stop 4 · Linkage 2 of 4

Net Income → top of Cash Flow

How it links

The other place Net Income lands: the very first line of the Cash Flow Statement. The CF reconciles the accrual-based Net Income on the IS back to actual cash movements.

From there, the CF adds non-cash items back (like Depreciation, +40) and adjusts for working capital changes (−20) to get to real Cash from Operations (233).

Stop 5 · Linkage 3 of 4

Ending Cash → Cash on the Balance Sheet

How it links

The Cash Flow ends with Ending Cash. That number flows back to the Balance Sheet as the new Cash balance. This is how the BS Cash line gets its value each year.

Notice both sides match — 250. That's the model "balancing." If your BS doesn't balance, this linkage is usually where it broke.

Stop 6 · Linkage 4 of 4

CapEx → PP&E

How it links

Companies buy new equipment, build new factories. That's Capital Expenditures on the CF. Those purchases land on the BS as part of PP&E (Property, Plant, and Equipment).

PP&E grows with CapEx and shrinks with Depreciation. The IS recognizes the wear-and-tear (Depreciation as an expense); the CF recognizes the cash spend (CapEx as an outflow). Same asset, two views.

The spine

That's the spine of every financial model

Net Income, Retained Earnings, Cash, and PP&E — those four lines plus their linkages are how every 3-statement model holds together. Add more line items, and the same logic carries through.

The 4 linkages
  • 1Net Income → Retained Earnings. Each year's profit accumulates on the BS.
  • 2Net Income → top of Cash Flow. Accrual profit becomes the starting point for cash.
  • 3Ending Cash → BS Cash. CF's bottom line lands as the new Cash balance.
  • 4CapEx → PP&E. Cash invested in assets shows up on the BS.
