Three quick checks, with the answers

  1. A donor gives Khan Academy $5 million to build a new physics course. Can that money also cover a security engineer's salary?
    No. That $5 million is restricted to the physics course it funded. Spending it on anything else breaks the terms of the gift, even though it is Khan Academy's money either way. This is exactly why earned money is different: it arrives with no strings on it.
  2. Which is worth more to the partnerships team: $1 million once, or $200,000 a year for five years?
    The five-year commitment. Same total dollars, different value. It behaves like a contract that renews: predictable, budgetable, and a signal the partner intends to stick around. A one-time payment might be the whole relationship, and then it is gone.
  3. Which of these does Khan Academy actually charge someone for? A student watching a math video, a school signing up for an Enterprise district license, a teacher opening Khanmigo at no cost to them, a parent paying $4 a month for a Khanmigo account.
    The district license and the parent's Khanmigo subscription are both charged. The video and the teacher's Khanmigo access are both free to the person using them, and paid for by someone else entirely.
