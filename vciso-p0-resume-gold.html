Open each line to see why a recruiter cares.

  • Defined and documented the scope of a security assessment, then built an asset inventory from two conflicting internal record sets, logging every gap and contradiction as a finding rather than silently picking one record over the other.

    Why this lands

    Interviewers always ask how you would begin. The answer they want is: write down what you are checking, then list everything inside it. Doing that from records that do not match is the hard part.

  • Mapped an organization's external attack surface using public sources only, including certificate transparency logs, DNS records and breach data, then triaged about 40 vulnerability scan results into 8 prioritized findings ranked by CISA KEV exploitation status rather than CVSS severity alone.

    Why this lands

    Ranking by what attackers are exploiting now, not by the score the scanner printed, is judgment most junior candidates cannot show. CISA KEV and CVSS are the terms recruiters and their screening software search for.

  • Tested documented controls and 8 client security claims against the evidence available, scored the assessed scope against all six NIST CSF 2.0 Functions at both current and target maturity, and consolidated 19 findings into 12 ranked risks, delivered as a client readout deck, a one-page executive summary and a completed customer security questionnaire.

    Why this lands

    NIST CSF 2.0 is named in a lot of risk and compliance job adverts. And anyone can list problems: handing leadership one ranked page they can act on is the part that gets you hired.
