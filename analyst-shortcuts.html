Analyst shortcuts

6 tricks that make you fast

These are workflow tips analysts use to write formulas faster — small things that compound over a model build.

Read each one, watch the demo, try it in your own sheet next time. Most analysts pick these up by watching a senior; here they are in one place.

What you'll learn:

  1. Click cells instead of typing them
  2. Reference cells across tabs
  3. F4 to toggle $ locks
  4. Drag the fill handle to copy formulas
  5. Format as % in one keystroke
  6. Copy formatting with the paint roller
Tip 1 of 6

Click, don't type

Don't manually type cell references. Once you've typed =, just click the cell you want — Sheets writes the reference for you. Works for any cell on any tab.

Use it when Always. It's faster, and you make fewer typos.

Tip 2 of 6

Pull values across tabs

Same trick works across sheets. Type =, click the other tab, click a cell — Sheets writes OtherTab!B3 for you. No manual typing of tab names.

Use it when Linking summary sheets to data tabs, pulling assumptions from a Drivers tab, etc.

Tip 3 of 6

F4 toggles $ signs

With your cursor inside a cell reference in a formula, press F4 (Windows) or ⌘+T (Mac). Each press cycles the lock: B1 → $B$1 → B$1 → $B1 → back to B1.

Use it when You're typing a formula and realize you need to lock the rate cell. No need to type $ signs manually.
Example: You typed =B7*G1 to compute 2021 tax (G1 holds the 21% rate). You're about to drag the formula right to fill in 2022, 2023, 2024 — but G1 will shift to H1, I1, J1 (empty cells) and break the model. Click G1 inside the formula bar, press F4, and it becomes $G$1. Now drag — the rate stays locked, the year reference moves correctly.

Tip 4 of 6

Drag the corner to fill

Every cell has a tiny square at its bottom-right corner — the fill handle. Drag it down or across to copy your formula. Cell references shift automatically (unless you locked them with $).

Use it when Filling formulas across years or down a column — never retype the same formula 4 times.

Tip 5 of 6

Format as % in one shortcut

Type your growth rate or margin formula and you'll get 0.125 not 12.5%. To fix it in one keystroke: select the cell and press Ctrl + Shift + 5 (Mac: ⌘ + Shift + 5). Cell flips to percentage format.

Use it when Anytime you see a decimal where you wanted a percentage.

Tip 6 of 6

Copy formatting with the Paint Roller

Need cell B5's formatting (color, font, bold, %, borders) applied to a whole row? Click B5, then click the paint roller icon on the toolbar, then click the target cells. Formatting copies over — no need to redo it manually.

Use it when Cleaning up a model that has inconsistent formatting from multiple contributors.
