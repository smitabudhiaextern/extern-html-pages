Grade four risk rows

Somebody else on your team has written four risk rows, one for each of four different deals. Every row is a stated risk paired with their stated answer to it. Your job is to grade each row before the page tells you anything. In these rows, we means the team you work on.

Commit first

Grade the row before you see any reasoning. A grade you committed to teaches you more than a verdict you read.

Three grades, not two

Keep as written, fix, or not a risk at all. Nothing here is scored and nothing is timed.

One row is finished

One of the four needs nothing done to it. Watch for which one, so you do not assume every row is broken.

Risk
Something that could go wrong inside this deal after both sides say yes.
Alternative
Something that could happen instead of this deal. A real thing, on a different line.
Mitigation
The specific thing you would do to make a risk less likely, or less damaging.
Renewal
The partner agreeing to carry on for another period once the first one ends.

Four ways to grade a risk row

Each row turned on a different one of these. Run all four on every risk row you write, in this order, including the ones you wrote yourself.

Rule one

Is it a risk at all?

A risk goes wrong inside the deal after both sides say yes. Something that could happen instead of the deal is an alternative, and it belongs on the line about who else could take this seat.

Rule two

Could you put a link next to it?

A risk is a claim, and a claim needs the same sourcing as a number. Cut the words that make the sentence bigger than your evidence.

Rule three

Does the answer name who does what before the meeting?

A person, an action, and a time. A reason to hurry is not a mitigation, however good a reason it is.

Rule four

Does the risk fit this deal rather than any deal?

The row worth writing down is the one this particular arrangement brings with it, not a worry you could paste onto anything.

Now write your own rows, two or three per deal, and grade each one four ways before it goes in.
