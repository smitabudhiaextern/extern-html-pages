Sample Code.org, a real organization Two models in, one out

Two money models for Code.org, and one left off on purpose

Code.org is a nonprofit that teaches computer science to school kids. Nonprofit means nobody owns it and nobody takes profit out of it. In 2026 it changed its name to CodeAI. Its courses are free, and its own site says more than 150 million students have used them. Free courses still cost money to make. So who pays?

$25.2 million came in last year. About 90 cents of every dollar was a gift.
From its public tax return, the year ended 31 August 2025 (Form 990, via ProPublica). The rest was mostly interest on money it already had.
Model 1 · borrowed from another company

Schools pay for the records. The courses stay free.

The model

Every student learns free, exactly as now. A school district, meaning the local office that runs a group of public schools, pays for what sits around the courses: enrollment records, progress reports, and one screen where it can see every classroom.

The deal it is based on On the record

Coursera does exactly this. Organizations paid it $255.3 million in the year ended 31 December 2025, and it had 1,730 paying organizations (Coursera, Form 10-K for the year ended 31 December 2025). Divide one by the other and the average organization pays about $147,600 a year. That division is ours, not Coursera's.

What Code.org gets

Money that comes back every school year, because districts set a budget every year. And the promise stays true: no student ever pays.

What could go wrong Our read

Schools buy slowly, and Code.org would need salespeople it does not have today. And if the reports cost money, teachers in schools that do not pay lose them. Teachers are the people Code.org most needs on its side.

Model 2 · Code.org has already done this

A company pays for a course, and its name goes on it.

The model

A company gives money so one course can be built. Its name appears on the course and in the news story about it. Students still pay nothing.

The deal it is based on On the record

Amazon gave Code.org $15 million over three years to build a new AP Computer Science A course, announced on 24 February 2021 (Amazon press release, 24 February 2021). Amazon gave the money. It did not buy anything, and no money flows back to it.

What Code.org gets

A big number pointed at one specific course, and proof for the next conversation. Once one company has signed, the second one is easier.

What could go wrong Our read

The company's name is on the course, so the company's problems can rub off on the course. And whoever pays for a course will have opinions about what goes in it. Who decides the content has to be written down before any money moves.

Left off · and why

Ads on the free courses. Out.

Duolingo does this. People who use it free watch ads, so the learner pays with attention instead of money. It is a real model with real money behind it, and it is still out.

The reason is specific to Code.org. Its learners are children, and its courses run inside classrooms. Schools block ads, and there are rules about advertising to children. The model works. It does not fit here.
