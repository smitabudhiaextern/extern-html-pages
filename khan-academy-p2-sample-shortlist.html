A money-model shortlist for Code.org

Code.org is a nonprofit that teaches computer science to school kids. Nonprofit means nobody owns it and nobody takes profit out of it. In 2026 it changed its name to CodeAI. Its courses are free, and its own site says more than 150 million students have used them. Free courses still cost money to make. So who pays?

$25.2 million came in last year. 90% of it was donations.
Donations 90% 10%
The 10% is mostly interest on money it already had. From its public tax return, the year ended 31 August 2025 (Form 990, via ProPublica).
Model 1 · borrowed from another company

Schools pay for the records. The courses stay free.

Every student learns free, exactly as now. A school district, meaning the local office that runs a group of public schools, pays for what sits around the courses: enrollment records, progress reports, and one screen where it can see every classroom.

The real deal it is based on On the record

Coursera does exactly this. Organizations paid it $255.3 million in the year ended 31 December 2025, and it had 1,730 paying organizations (Coursera, Form 10-K for the year ended 31 December 2025). Divide one by the other and the average organization pays about $147,600 a year.

What Code.org would get

Money it can count on. Schools plan their spending once a year, every year, so a school that pays this year is likely to pay next year too. And students still pay nothing.

What could go wrong Our read

Schools take a long time to say yes, and Code.org would need salespeople it does not have today. And if the reports cost money, teachers in schools that do not pay lose them. Teachers are the people Code.org most needs on its side.

Model 2 · Code.org has already done this

A company pays for a course, and its name goes on it.

A company gives money so one course can be built. Its name appears on the course and in the news story about it. Students still pay nothing.

The real deal it is based on On the record

Amazon gave Code.org $15 million over three years to build a new AP Computer Science A course, announced on 24 February 2021 (Amazon press release, 24 February 2021). Amazon gave the money as a donation. It did not buy anything, and no money flows back to it.

What Code.org would get

A large amount of money for one specific course, and proof for the next conversation. Once one company has signed, the second one is easier to convince.

What could go wrong Our read

The company's name is on the course. If the company ends up in bad news, people will see its name on the course too. And a company that pays for a course will have opinions about what goes in it, so who decides the content must be agreed in writing before any money arrives.
