Project 4 · Step 2 · Pattern Detection

Something Doesn't Add Up:
Spotting Anomalies

The senior analyst's superpower is noticing that something's off before you know exactly what. Five checks follow. Copy each query, run it in DB Browser, and look at the shape of what comes back.

Pattern 1

The distribution that's too flat

Real e-commerce traffic has clear daily and weekly rhythms: busy at lunch, busy in the evening, quiet overnight. If you GROUP BY hour and every hour comes back with nearly the same count, that flatness itself is the red flag. It's not normal customer behavior. It's automation.

You saw this query previously, in the last module (4.2.1)
Carts by hour of day 
SELECT
    substr(created_at, 12, 2) AS hour_of_day,
    COUNT(DISTINCT name) AS carts_started
FROM shopify_checkouts
GROUP BY hour_of_day
ORDER BY hour_of_day;
On Breaking Games' real data this looks healthy: carts peak around lunchtime and evening, and go quiet overnight. That's the pattern you want to see: one class of automation ruled out.

Pattern 2

The same email, too many times

A repeat customer is normal. A repeat customer with 30 carts and zero purchases is not.

Repeat carts by email 
SELECT
    email,
    COUNT(DISTINCT name) AS cart_count
FROM shopify_checkouts
WHERE email IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY email
HAVING cart_count > 5
ORDER BY cart_count DESC
LIMIT 20;
Comes back empty here: the busiest email in the export has 3 carts. A legitimate finding, not a broken query: you've just ruled out a whole class of bot behavior.

Pattern 3

Cart values that don't make sense

Carts with extreme lineitem_quantity (6+ of the same product) deserve a second look. Sometimes it's a real reseller; most of the time it's automation or test data.

Carts with 6+ of one item 
SELECT
    name, email, lineitem_name, lineitem_quantity, total
FROM shopify_checkouts
WHERE lineitem_quantity > 5
ORDER BY lineitem_quantity DESC;
This query returns 30 rows, topping out at the real max of 12: customer_1009@example.com's cart, two line items of "GAME of PHONES," both quantity 12. Notice total is blank on that cart's second line item: only the first line item of a multi-item cart carries the cart-level total.

Pattern 4

Carts that violate physical constraints

If a cart was created at 3:47:01 AM and another by the same email at 3:47:09 AM in a different country, that's not a human. A single email tied to multiple billing countries is almost always test data or fraud.

Countries per email 
SELECT
    email,
    COUNT(DISTINCT billing_country) AS countries,
    COUNT(DISTINCT name) AS carts
FROM shopify_checkouts
WHERE email IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY email
HAVING countries > 1
ORDER BY countries DESC;
This query comes back empty: no single email is tied to more than one billing country in this data. That's a legitimate finding, not a broken query, you've just ruled out a whole class of fraud/test-data behavior. Write it down and move on.

Pattern 5

Data older than the story it's telling

Every table has a time range, and it rarely matches the question you're asking. Check before you trust:

Carts by year 
SELECT substr(created_at, 1, 4) AS year, COUNT(DISTINCT name) AS carts
FROM shopify_checkouts
GROUP BY year
ORDER BY year;
Confirmed on the real data: carts reach back to 2016, 2021 spikes to 272 carts, and 551 of the 1,062 total carts (52%) predate 2025. Any rate computed against this year's orders was never comparing like with like.

Okay, it's a real finding. Now prove it.

A reusable 3-step dig

Once you've confirmed a finding is real and material, dig deeper with sequential queries. Replace [pattern] with your condition, e.g. lineitem_quantity > 5.

Forensic 3-step template 
-- Step 1: How widespread is the pattern?
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM shopify_checkouts WHERE [pattern];

-- Step 2: Who exhibits it?
SELECT email, COUNT(*) FROM shopify_checkouts WHERE [pattern]
GROUP BY email ORDER BY 2 DESC;

-- Step 3: What's the impact if we exclude it?
SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT name) FROM shopify_checkouts WHERE NOT [pattern];

Three queries, one pattern, a clear before-and-after.
