The Breaking Games Externship

A 5-minute pitch for someone who isn't technical

⏱️ Total: about 5 minutes · 7 short beats
💡 How to use this: each box is roughly 40 seconds. Read it once to learn the flow, then keep it open while you talk.

Start here: what it actually is

⏱️ 0:00 – 0:40 · The hook

"Picture a real company that makes board games. They're growing fast — but all their important information (what sold, who bought it, which ads ran) is scattered across messy spreadsheets that don't talk to each other. Nobody can answer simple questions like 'are our ads even working?'"

"In this 8-week externship, the student becomes the company's data detective. Week by week they untangle the mess, and at the end they hand the founder a real 'Holiday Game Plan' — with their name on it. Here's what each of the five projects covers."

The five projects, one at a time

1

Cleaning up the mess

⏱️ 0:40 – 1:20

"The company's info lives in six separate lists that take hours to search by hand. The student pours them all into one organized place — a database — and learns to ask it questions and get answers in seconds instead of hours."

In one line: From six messy spreadsheets to one tidy place you can actually ask.
2

Making everything connect

⏱️ 1:20 – 2:00

"There's a catch: the same game is written differently on every list, so the computer thinks one game is five different games. The student gives each game a single ID tag so the lists finally connect — then uses that to tell the boss which games to reorder for the holidays."

In one line: Give everything an ID tag so the data finally connects.
3

Did the ads actually work?

⏱️ 2:00 – 2:40

"The company spent $37,000 on Facebook and Instagram ads but can't tell which ones actually brought in sales. The student sorts the traffic, does the money math on each ad, and hands over a plan: keep these ads, stop those, spend the rest here."

In one line: Follow the money — which ads paid off, which wasted cash.
4

The vanishing shoppers

⏱️ 2:40 – 3:20

"Over a thousand people filled their carts and left without buying. The twist: some of those 'shoppers' are actually bots — fake robot visitors messing up the numbers. The student filters out the bots and finds the real story of why customers leave."

In one line: Separate real customers from bots to find the true numbers.
5

The big presentation

⏱️ 3:20 – 4:05

"Finally, the student turns everything into one simple dashboard — a single page with charts — and presents it to the whole team, including the founder, right before Black Friday. They have to explain it clearly and defend it when people push back."

In one line: Turn all the answers into one clear dashboard and present it.
⏱️ 4:05 – 5:00 · The closer

Why it's impressive 🏆

"By the end, the student hasn't just learned tools — they've done the real job: take messy data, find the truth, and tell a story that helps a business make money."

"They walk away with a finished project they can show in interviews, plus hands-on skills — databases, analytics, and modern AI tools — used the same way real analysts work today."

"That's the whole journey: from a pile of messy spreadsheets to a plan the founder acts on. 🎲"

Quick answers if they ask 🙋

"Is this just learning to code?"

Not really — it's learning to answer business questions with data. Code is just the tool, and AI does a lot of the heavy lifting.

"Is it made-up data, or real?"

It's a realistic company scenario with genuinely messy, real-world-style data — the same kind of problems an actual analyst faces on day one.

"What do they actually walk away with?"

A finished "Holiday Game Plan" they can show in interviews, plus real skills in databases, analytics, and AI tools like Claude.
