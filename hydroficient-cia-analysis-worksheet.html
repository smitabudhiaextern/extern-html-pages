CIA Analysis of Grand Marina Assets

Marcus Webb just asked: "Where should we spend our security budget first?" "Everywhere" isn't an answer. Work through the three sections, then generate your submission block and paste it into the platform.

💾 Your work auto-saves in this browser — you can close the tab and come back.

1 · Rate each asset (1 = low importance · 5 = critical)

For each asset, rate how much its Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability matter — then justify in one sentence. Think: what happens if this property fails? A burst pipe affects guests; occupancy data affects privacy.

2 · Map one specific attack scenario for each attack type

Be specific — don't write "attacker gets data." Describe how they do it and what they get. The example row shows the level of detail expected.

3 · Rank the six attacks — most to least dangerous for the Grand Marina

Weigh likelihood (how easy to pull off?) against impact (how bad if it succeeds?). A high-impact-but-unlikely attack may rank below a medium-impact-but-easy one. There is no single right answer — be ready to defend yours.

🧠 Can I use AI? Yes — but don't start there.

Complete at least one row of each section yourself first. Then use AI to pressure-test, with prompts like:

  • "I rated Emergency Shutoff as C:3, I:5, A:5 for a hotel water system. What am I missing?"
  • "Here's my replay-attack scenario: [paste]. Is it specific and realistic enough?"
  • "I ranked Unauthorized Access most dangerous because [reasoning]. What factors am I overlooking?"

If you can't explain why an answer makes sense, it isn't yours yet. "The AI told me" won't survive a conversation with Marcus Webb.
