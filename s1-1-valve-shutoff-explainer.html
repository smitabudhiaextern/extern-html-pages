So far, the system only watches. The watchers read the water, the postman carries the readings, the screen shows green. But the system does not just watch. It can also control the water: slow it down, or stop it completely.
Part 1 of 2
The Tap (Gate Valve)
Every station has a tap on its pipe, the same idea as the tap on your sink, just bigger. The hotel can open it wider, close it a little, or shut it completely. And nobody walks to the basement to turn it. It is done from far away, by sending a command.
Sending a Command
A Command Travels the Wire
Remember the wire that carries readings up to the screen? A command can travel the same wire in the other direction. A command is just a short message, like a text. This one says close the tap. The valve reads it and does what it says.
Part 2 of 2
The Big Red Button (Emergency Shutoff)
There is one special command for emergencies: the shutoff. One click, and the water to a whole building stops at once. It exists for good reasons. If a pipe bursts at 3 AM, the hotel does not want to hunt for the right valve in the dark.
One Last Thing
Worth Remembering
The valve does not know who is asking. It obeys whatever valid command arrives on the wire. That little fact is going to matter soon.
The Whole System
The Whole System Now
Now you've met the whole system. Readings flow up to the screen. Commands flow down to the taps. Everything the hotel knows, and everything it can do, travels on these wires.
