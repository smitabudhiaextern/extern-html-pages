The Shape of a Finished Threat Model

Maya hands you a real threat model from a past client: MedVault, patient monitors at Riverside General Hospital. Different client, same shape as the one you will build for the Grand Marina. You are not reading the whole thing now. You are learning the shape: six sections, each with one job.

Section 1 of 6

Executive Summary

This section is for the person who signs the budget. It says what was found and why it matters, in plain words, with no jargon.

From the MedVault threat model
1Weak authentication on the clinical dashboard could allow unauthorized access to patient data and alert settings. Critical
What to notice

It never mentions STRIDE. It names three critical findings in words a hospital administrator understands: a weak dashboard login, unencrypted device traffic, and one server that takes down all 50 devices if it fails.

Section 2 of 6

System Overview

This section explains what the system is and how data flows through it, so any reader can follow the rest of the report.

From the MedVault threat model
MedVault Devices→ Local Gateway→ MedVault Cloud→ Clinical Dashboard
What to notice

A simple picture beats paragraphs. Your Step 2 map of the Grand Marina is already this section, nearly finished.

Section 3 of 6

Asset Inventory

This section lists what is worth protecting, with a Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability priority for each asset.

From the MedVault threat model
Patient vital sign readings · Integrity: Critical
What to notice

Every rating comes with a reason. Wrong data causes wrong treatment, so Integrity is Critical here. Your Vault work is the same move.

Section 4 of 6

STRIDE Analysis

This is the core. Each component is checked against all six threat families, and each threat gets a Scenario, a Likelihood, an Impact, and a Risk.

From the MedVault threat model
Tampering: an attacker with dashboard access raises the heart-rate alert threshold from 120 to 200 BPM.
Likelihood: Medium Impact: Critical Risk: Critical
What to notice

Every scenario is specific. It names who does what, to which part, with what result. "Attacker does bad thing" never appears.

The sample covers four components in full depth. Your build goes deep on one component and lighter on the rest. The builder walks you through it.

Section 5 of 6

Risk Summary

This section is the ranked list: every finding, worst first, one line each.

From the MedVault threat model
Unencrypted device communications, vitals cross the WiFi with no protection. Critical
Patient data export, bulk export allowed with no extra authorization. High
Alert interception, alert messages reveal who is critically ill. Medium
What to notice

A busy reader who reads nothing else still leaves knowing what to fix first. Critical, then High, then Medium.

Section 6 of 6

Recommended Mitigations

For every big risk, this section proposes a fix and says how hard it is to put in place.

From the MedVault threat model
Multi-factor authentication for all dashboard users · closes a Critical risk · Complexity: Low
What to notice

The best rows pair a big risk with a cheap fix. Multi-factor authentication is Low complexity and closes a Critical risk. That is what a decision-maker acts on the same day.
