09 / 20 · SETUP · WORKING CAPITAL
Category · Working Capital Driver 4 of 8 (two parts)

Working Capital · DSO + DPO

Two timing numbers — how long until you get paid, and how long you take to pay. On Maya's cash-and-carry stand, both are zero. That changes the moment she signs her first café account.

Part A · DSO
Days Sales Outstanding
How long after the sale until cash arrives?
DSO = (Accounts Receivable ÷ Revenue) × 365
0days · Summer 1
Drives: accounts receivable on the balance sheet, and a cash outflow when DSO rises. Higher DSO = more revenue tied up waiting.

This matters when Maya adds a café customer that pays in 30 days. Revenue stays the same; cash arrives a month later.
Part B · DPO
Days Payable Outstanding
How long do you take to pay your suppliers?
DPO = (Accounts Payable ÷ COGS) × 365
0days · Summer 1
Drives: accounts payable on the balance sheet, and a cash inflow when DPO rises. Higher DPO = supplier funds your operations longer.

This matters when Maya's lemon supplier offers net-30 terms. Same COGS; cash leaves a month later.
One way to think about it: DSO − DPO ≈ how many days your business funds itself out of pocket. Negative number = customers pay you before suppliers do (a great place to be).

15 / 20 · SETUP · INTEREST
Category · Capital & Tax Driver 7 of 8

Interest Rate

Maya's aunt loaned her $50 to help buy the cart, on the gentle condition that Maya pay 10% interest at the end of the summer. That $5 is the cost of borrowing.

$50 × 10% = $5
Formula Interest Expense = Average Debt Balance × Interest Rate Drives The interest expense line that sits below operating income, before taxes. Higher debt or rate = lower net income. Your job Maya pays 10%. If you assume the company refinances at 6%, you're betting credit markets cooperate. Tie the rate to actual loan terms in the filings.

17 / 20 · SETUP · TAX
Category · Capital & Tax Driver 8 of 8
Maya has no taxes this summer. For your real company, you'll need a rate.
Pre-tax Income × Tax Rate = Taxes
Most U.S. public companies pay an effective tax rate near 25% once you blend federal, state, and a few credits — though the number varies by industry. Pull the last 2–3 years of effective rate from the income statement and use that as your forecast assumption.

Drives: the gap between pre-tax income and net income — the bottom line you compare across companies.
