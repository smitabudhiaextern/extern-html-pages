Part 1 of 2 · Find the Threats

Every Attack Has a Home

Think about a grocery store. It stocks thousands of products, but only a handful of aisles: dairy, produce, frozen, snacks. A new worker never memorizes every product. They learn the aisles. After that they can shelve anything, even a snack that came out last week, because every product belongs in one aisle.

Security works the same way. There are thousands of attacks, far too many to memorize. But every one belongs in one of six aisles, and that set of six is called STRIDE. The six attacks you ran in the Attack Lab are six products you have already handled. In a minute you'll shelve them into their aisles, and you'll find you already know five of the six.

Tampering

Changing data or a command while it is on the move, so the screen shows something that is not true.

Sorting Tells You What Can Happen. Not What to Fix First.

For that, professionals score every threat with two questions:

How likely is it?
Low · Medium · High
How bad would it be?
Low · Medium · High · Critical
Likelihood × Impact = Risk

Notice the mismatch: likelihood has 3 levels, impact has 4. Impact gets an extra level, Critical, because some failures are so catastrophic that "high" isn't a big enough word for them.
